## Lead: the strait is being mined while the price falls

The IRGC navy says an LNG tanker struck a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz this morning on an "unauthorised and dangerous route." That follows Thursday's claim that four oil tankers were hit by explosions in or near the waterway, and the earlier tanker fires the Guards attributed to unauthorised routing. Riyadh and Washington have not confirmed any of it. Iran is announcing its own mine strikes.

**Now the fact that matters more than the attacks.** Brent is around $95, down 2.4% on the week after falling 16% the previous week; the IEA reports the first crude surplus since the Iran war began. Reuters' framing this morning is explicit: prices are headed for a second weekly decline "as the continued US blockade of Iran eased fears of renewed fighting."

Read that against Wednesday's declaration — *"We've got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we've got their Strait."* **The market has priced the blockade as containment, not as possession and not as escalation.** The waterway is being mined by the party the President says he has stripped of it, and crude is falling because traders read the blockade as the thing preventing a wider war rather than as a war-winning instrument. Two readings of the same policy are in circulation and they cannot both be right: either the strait is held, in which case the mines are an embarrassment, or the strait is contested, in which case "we've got it" is a claim about leverage rather than about water.

**This is evidence against the near-term escalation branch, and I am not repricing on it.** The Iran escalation card (Cenk step 2, "a much bigger war during the lame duck") sits at 18 and this morning's tape is consistent with that number rather than a reason to move it: declared escalation rhetoric, contained physical violence, softening price, first surplus since the war began. The card's falsifier — a named operational phase, expanded aims, or a formal war-opening act before the 120th Congress is seated — is untouched. Mine strikes on tankers are escalation of a kind; they are not the kind the card names, and the market's own read is that the two sides have settled into a violent equilibrium. Recorded as evidence, not resolution.

**The sanctions file cuts the same way.** Today's Federal Register publishes three Iran-related OFAC general licenses — GLs EE, FF and W. These are wind-down authorisations for previously blocked persons and vessels (EE issued 8 October for Samudra Marine Services, FF amended 29 October for International Development Bank, W published today). **They are the paperwork of continued blocking, not relief**, and they are being published in the Federal Register now because OFAC publishes web licences after the fact. Anyone reading "Iran general licences" this morning as sanctions easing should read the licence titles. The architecture is still a blockade with wind-down windows.

## 1. Nvidia: the board removed the bottleneck in nine days, and the state is arriving afterwards

Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Thursday. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO; the board has begun a search and will consider outsiders. Roughly $550bn of market value has gone with him, and Reuters reports $3bn of Chinese AI projects reliant on Nvidia capacity are now imperilled, with Washington's scrutiny as the proximate cause. The New York Times reports that the President had urged Huang to fight for his job before the board acted.

**The sequence is the finding, and it is now complete.** 11-03: the Alice Huang matter surfaces (former Megaspeed director, assessed by US intelligence as an MSS asset). 11-11: three House chairs seek a closed-door appearance. 11-12: the board removes him. 11-12, afternoon: Senate Banking chair Tim Scott writes to Huang and to Commerce Secretary Lutnick summoning both, Huang on **3 December**, Lutnick after, with subpoenas threatened. Lutnick has agreed to appear.

So: a private board removed the principal of the AI supply chain's bottleneck in nine days, before any regulator moved, and the state's first compulsory process is a hearing scheduled **after** the removal, three weeks out, in public. The export-control question the case actually raises — that capacity is being rented abroad rather than chips shipped — is still unanswered, and the person best placed to answer it will now be answering questions about an affair instead. **The removal settled the market question and buried the policy question.** A public hearing is better than the closed-door one the House wanted, and it is still arriving after the only decision that mattered has been made.

Watch: whether Scott's hearing reaches remote access and capacity rental; whether Lutnick's appearance produces a Commerce rule; whether the permanent CEO search is a domestic-only slate; whether any criminal referral follows.

## 2. Mississippi was called, and nobody refused to count it

AP called the Mississippi Senate race for Scott Colom on Wednesday after Jackson County posted 2,173 additional votes; Hyde-Smith conceded the same afternoon. Final shape: 49.2 to 48.7, a margin that moved from 43 votes at midnight to roughly 4,700–5,600 by the call, with provisional ballots supplying the shift. Colom becomes Mississippi's first Black senator since Reconstruction.

**The legitimacy finding is a null, and nulls are the point.** The count-refusal card at 40 asks whether a named Republican refuses a count by 31 December. In the one race where the machinery had every opportunity — a narrow margin, a week of counting, provisional ballots deciding it, a Black Democrat winning a state the President carried — the incumbent conceded. The count was not contested. The county-by-county tally ran in public through a state election management system and the losing party accepted it on the day the last county reported.

That is a data point for the null, not proof of safety: refusal is a decision made by an actor with something to gain, and Hyde-Smith had nothing to gain from a fight she was losing by thousands. **It gets recorded because the base rate for the next one is built out of these.** The Alaska case remains the live test, where a court has already declined to order the disclosure on capacity grounds.

## 3. The vote that passed because eleven Democrats were absent

House Republicans referred Massie's Hegseth impeachment resolution to Judiciary on 10 November, 211-204. Eleven Democrats were absent. The arithmetic is unforgiving: 204 plus 11 is 215, and the referral fails. A Democratic member apologised publicly this week for missing it, saying no one is more disappointed in him than himself.

Two things follow. **First, the outcome was produced by attendance, not by argument.** A 215-211 loss on a procedural referral would have kept the articles live on the floor; a 211-204 win killed them for the Congress. Nobody has to have been bought or pressured for that to be true — a Tuesday evening vote decided by who was in the building is a normal legislative fact, and it is also the cheapest possible way to win one.

**Second, the apology is the wrong compensation.** The member is apologising for the one failure that has a fix — being present — in a week when the underlying question (eight articles, command responsibility, the Iran operation) was buried without a vote on its merits. Hegseth remains, the removal card sits at 55 with the seating of the 120th Congress as its gate, and the only live route back to the floor is a discharge petition on H RES 1655, which still has no filing with named signatories.

Also in the corpus and worth one line: Hegseth awarded the Joint Service Commendation Medal to Spc. Thomas Iglesias at the Pentagon, three days after Iglesias told the Secretary's party to be quiet at the Tomb. Co-optation executed cleanly and cheaply — no inquiry, no protocol change, no officer on the record. The open item from Wednesday — whether any serving officer says anything publicly about the Secretary's conduct — remains open and unanswered.

## 4. Mexico: the caravan arrives on schedule, and the tariff is the instrument

A caravan of more than a thousand people is walking north through Chiapas, one week after the election. The President threatened Thursday to double tariffs on Mexico if it is not stopped, adding that they will not be allowed to enter. Sheinbaum says no caravan has reached the northern border since he took office, and that she will use persuasion rather than force.

**The instrument is the tell.** The demand is migration enforcement; the lever chosen is the tariff, i.e. a commercial punishment applied to a sovereign for a policing outcome it has already been delivering on. Every prior round of this resolved the same way — Mexican enforcement increases, the tariff is withdrawn as a win, and the precedent that trade access is revocable at the President's discretion is renewed. That precedent is the durable output, not the caravan. Caravans are useful because they are visible, seasonal, and always partly composed of people who will turn back; a manufactured crisis needs a photograph, and a thousand people walking is one.

Watch: whether the tariff is actually proclaimed (a Federal Register notice, not a post), whether Mexico deploys enforcement north of the Suchiate, and whether the 100% figure survives contact with US importers.
