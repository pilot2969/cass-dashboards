## Lead: the bank of Nvidia posted a quarter, and its board closed the door

**The numbers were enormous and the guidance was better.** Nvidia's fiscal Q3: revenue **$115.84 billion, up 103%** year on year; adjusted EPS **$2.68, up 111%**; data centre **$107.6 billion** against $101.85 billion expected. Q4 guided to **$127.4–132.6 billion** against a $125.25 billion consensus. A **$150 billion buyback**. Fiscal 2028 growth guidance raised to **75% from 70%**. The stock closed down 0.44% and rose 1.46% overnight.

**And the board used the same call to end the personnel question.** Chair Harvey Jones read a statement: Huang "voluntarily resigned," the board "unanimously accepted" it and "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO." A search firm has been retained; interim CEO Colette Kress stays until a permanent hire. The company also stood up a **National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee** — independent directors, outside advisers, counsel — the morning of the call. Jones then refused all questions about the resignation and the investigation.

**That refusal is the answer to the President.** Trump said Monday he "would not mind" seeing Huang restored and had "thrown out the name of Tim Cook." The board heard it, thanked him, and closed the door in a prepared statement it would not elaborate on. A company under a national-security investigation has decided its defence is a board committee and an audit trail, not a founder.

**Now read the balance sheet, because the call volunteered it.** Nvidia's total committed, guaranteed or invested capital reached roughly **$670 billion**, up from $530 billion in July. It **guarantees $118 billion of other companies' obligations**, including nearly $10 billion new to unnamed partners, and bought nearly **$16 billion of stakes** in other firms. Its **unpaid bills are $81 billion — 64 days of sales**, the slowest since the 2022 slump. The cloud backlog it cites is **$2.5 trillion**, up from $2 trillion three months ago. Kress called the funding guarantees "disciplined and selective," and said the company is "rigorously evaluating" future commitments.

**That is a credit institution's disclosure, in a chip company's 10-Q.** Revenue is real; so is the fact that the growth is being financed by the seller and paid for late by the buyers. When one firm guarantees its customers' obligations, holds equity in them, and carries 64 days of receivables, a demand shock does not travel through the order book — it travels through the guarantor in one step.

**And Anthropic prices into that structure tomorrow.** Orders are reported at roughly **six times the shares**; CNBC's David Faber reported Tuesday the deal is likely to price **above** the $70–78 range; offshore perps are quoting near **$99**. 825 million shares, pricing Wednesday, trading Thursday as ANT, on a net loss above **$104 billion** for the nine months to September. Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC are buying the private placement at the offer price.

**Class gap (F) is no longer a gap in our notes; it is the largest unpriced fact of the week.** The suppliers are underwriting the float of the customer, the customer's chip vendor is guaranteeing the cloud builders, and the buyers of both are each other. We still hold no collection for the capital plumbing of the AI build-out, and tonight it produced the clearest specimen the corpus has seen: a $2.5 trillion backlog disclosed by the party guaranteeing the loans that pay for it.
