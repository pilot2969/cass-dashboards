## Lead: the airspace stopped for four hours, and the explanation arrived before the story did

A national ground stop ran from roughly 16:30 to 20:28 ET after air traffic control centres lost contact with aircraft. Twenty-two centres were affected. It is the **third national ground stop in US aviation history** — 2001, 11 January 2023, tonight. The 2023 event lasted about ninety minutes and was caused by a contractor deleting files during a database synchronisation; tonight's ran four hours.

**The cause, as of filing.** CBS News, multiple sources: an update to the private communications network carrying ATC data caused equipment to reboot and the system to fail. That network is FTI — the FAA Telecommunications Infrastructure — operated by **L3Harris**, not a public carrier. The podium corroborates the mechanism without naming the vendor: Duffy said the switches and routers "got overloaded with data" and began returning inconsistent data, and that the FAA shut the system down because of it; Mullin said new equipment was "overwhelming the older system." No cyberattack, said flatly twice, by DHS and by law enforcement sources.

**What tonight actually produced, in order of durability:**

1. **A vendor-transport failure at the layer beneath the agency.** The FAA's own systems were healthy; the plumbing it buys was not. This was the correct branch and it was under-weighted in my own afternoon distribution, which had "carrier or cloud" at 0.15 while the answer sat one level below — the vendor that *runs* the FAA's private backbone. Same structural mistake twice in one day: I keep locating the dependency at the commercial-ISP layer because that is the layer with visible headlines.
2. **An instant funding argument.** Both secretaries converted an operational failure into a $50bn modernisation demand from the podium the same evening. That is exploitation of an occasion, and it is on tape. Whether the occasion was manufactured is a separate claim, now pre-registered with four falsifiers on the specimen record (see Watch).
3. **A four-hour information vacuum in primetime.** The distinguishing feature against 2023 is not the media environment — 2023 had the same cyberattack speculation and a *longer* root-cause lag (nine days). It is that 2023 happened 07:20–09:00, before the news day opened, and tonight happened across drive-time to primetime. A vacuum has to be experienced while awake to fill. Tonight it filled with eleven artefacts in the owner's capture alone: two false (a "national radar outage" that NWS denied and live products refuted; a fabricated "RADIOS DOWN" city map), one scope-distorted (the 9 October SMART AI oversight letter, which concerned three airports, not the 22-centre national rollout), one stale (a Fox "Jordan missile attack" post dated 30 August), one true coincidence welded in (the New Orleans paddlewheel collision, 32 hospitalised, genuine maritime accident, no aviation link), and one true observation misread (small props departing).

A demand curve is what a national flight halt *should* produce. A service degrading under redirected demand is the same data point echoing, not a new one.
