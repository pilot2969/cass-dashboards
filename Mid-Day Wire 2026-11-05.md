## 1. CORRECTION TO MY OWN MORNING READ: the AI unwind is political, not thematic
This morning I read the selloff as a single-theme AI capex unwind with no macro content, and said so. Intraday the tape has named its own cause, and it is the election. Reuters, CNN Business, Yahoo: chip weakness after the Democratic sweep, on fear of tougher AI regulation and slower data-centre buildout in the states that flipped. Nasdaq -5.3% intraday and into correction (10% off high); SOX ~-10%; Nvidia -7.7%; Palantir ~-$40B, worst day since February; Dow -1,300pts, giving back the 3% post-midterm day entirely.
The distinguishing detail, and it is the one I had wrong: data-centre siting is a STATE and LOCAL permitting question. Virginia, Georgia, Ohio, Texas, Arizona — that is where the buildout is, and those are the jurisdictions that changed hands. So the market is not pricing a federal regulatory bill it cannot pass. It is pricing the layer where the actual gate sits, which just became Democratic. That is a real repricing, not a mood.
What it is not: a recession signal. Rates and credit are not confirming; this is one sector's discount rate moving on a change in permitting risk.

## 3. PROCEDURE LEG — first empirical result of the postmortem forecast, and it splits the states
The postmortem ask was: enumerate which channels each newly-losing majority actually controls, and predict which gets used first. First sweep with real data. The answer is not uniform, and the reason is countable.
TEXAS — channel OPEN, and it is a simple majority. Republicans hold 19 of 31 Senate seats (Dems 12). Chamber rules are adopted by simple majority at session open, and the lieutenant governor's power sits almost entirely in those rules, not in the constitution. The GOP caucus met this afternoon on exactly that. Constraint: Art. IV §9 makes the LG the president of the senate, so the floor is "presides" — the product will be a hollowed office, not an abolished one. Precedent: NC 2016, WI 2018.
OHIO — channel CLOSED, and closed by arithmetic rather than by choice. House supermajority is GONE (42 D / 52 R; 60 needed), Senate supermajority HELD (22 R / 11 D). An Ohio veto override requires three-fifths of BOTH chambers. So the lame duck can pass anything it likes and incoming Gov. Acton can veto it, and it cannot be overridden. Further: DeWine has said he does not plan to sign anything limiting the governor's authority. The 136th GA returns for its final days and the structural ceiling on what it can entrench is already fixed.
FEDERAL EXECUTIVE — channel OPEN and already in use: the Alaska allocation demand, the Dhillon exit, the pre-3-January confirmation squeeze.
ORDERING, stated for the record: Texas first (moving today), federal executive ongoing, Ohio last and largely incapable — so the interesting Ohio question is not what the legislature does but what an outgoing majority does with agency rulemaking before 12 January, which is the one lever Turcer flagged that survives a veto.
