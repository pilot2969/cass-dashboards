2.2 Micro · what feeds each leg
Four anchors per leg, each scored 0 to 5 on one written scale, and load = (sum of anchors ÷ 4) × 20. An anchor is something you can go and find, not a judgement about how bad things feel. The legs read in model order: M, G, E, T.
The computed column is what the anchors produce; the live board column is the hand-set number it would replace. Where they differ, the anchor reading is the one with a paper trail — and T's gap is the reason this instrument exists.

3 · Absorption velocity — the other thing the instrument could not see

Section 2 measures stocks: holdings, hand-set once a month. This measures a flow: how fast dehumanizing frames move through the feed, counted continuously. The two answer different questions. A stock says how captured an institution is today; a flow says whether the audience that would have to accept force is being prepared faster than it was last week. The second is the leading indicator, and it never belonged in the dial table — you cannot defend "dehumanization is 71" from public observables, but you can defend "33 specimens this week, 45 coded right-flank, 25 reciprocal."

3.4 Against the model — is Absorption converting?
The model reads Absorption at 76 on a stock basis. The flow says the rate stepped up 4× into the election and did not return to baseline after it. That combination is the one the stock cannot express: high stock, rising flow means the frame is being refreshed, not merely retained. It is the leading edge of the M reading, and it is the only reading on this page that updates daily.
How to read it, plainly. Big number, bad. Rising, worse. Rising after a defeat means the frame is being used to explain the defeat rather than to contest it — the cheap version, and it still works. Falling after a defeat is the repair succeeding. The blind spot is on the panel, not hidden: capture is opportunistic. Samuel captures what the feed serves him. The rate is indicative, not a census, and the direction split is trustworthy only because the watch is deliberately symmetric. Do not quote a specimen count as a percentage of anything.
What would falsify this block. If a full-feed sample, not the owner's feed, shows the W41 peak is a sampler artefact, the rate series is void and only the direction coding survives. Until then the rate is a trend line on a non-random sample, labelled as one.

4 · Institutional coverage — where the dial set has holes

Eight dials was not a list of institutions, it was a list of the ones that happened to be scored. Audited against four admission rules, and the audit found real gates and real capture targets missing from the set:
RuleTest
R1 · GateDoes it sit astride a decision another actor must clear to complete a transfer of function? A gate is a veto, not an opinion.
R2 · Capture targetWould its compliance decide whether an enforcement action actually lands on the street, or whether a claim can be published at all?

The series break nobody would notice

Adding dials changes the mean by arithmetic, not by news. Media at 84, State Governments at 70, Local Law Enforcement at 74 and Arts and Expression at 83 take the set from 8 to 12: the mean moves 78 → 78 (627 ÷ 8 = 78.4; 938 ÷ 12 = 78.2 — it rounds back to the same integer), while dials at Critical falls from 5 of 8 to 7 of 12, or 63% to 58%. Nothing got calmer and nothing got worse. Two Warning dials diluted the share; one further Critical dial restored part of it.
Rule adopted: every reading stores its set version (8-dial-2026-10 → 12-dial-2026-11) and no mean or Critical share is ever compared across a set change. A mean published without a set version is a FAIL at the gate. The twelve-dial set is the baseline from today; every future addition is a dated recalibration, never a quiet append.

What the audit could not have found, and how Arts got in

The candidate list above was generated by a frame, and the frame had a bias: every name on it holds either a formal veto (a court, a certification, a governor's Guard authorisation) or a licence (a broadcast authorisation, an accreditation, an appropriation). Institutions whose power is legitimation rather than procedure — a stage, a museum, a schedule, a canon — hold neither, so R1 failed them on the way in and the audit never reached the question of whether they matter.
Arts and Expression was not found by the audit. It was named, and it had to be, because the case is already in the record and none of it is speculative: a federal court refused to inscribe the president's name on the Kennedy Center, holding that Congress holds naming authority; the board he controls voted to shut the building hours later, citing a structural emergency and a $23M deficit, with the stated remedy being his name on the facade to secure promised funding; fencing went up the next day and a sworn declaration describes a forklift running into the pillars; the $257M congressionally approved renovation was linked publicly to "recognition." Judge Cooper then imposed thirty days' notice before demolition plus a sworn status report. That is a captured flagship, a conditioned money valve, and an executive in open compliance-and-continue around an expressive institution — scored 83, Critical, below Civil Service at 90 and Education at 86, above the courts at 81.
What keeps it under 90: the naming ruling held; the notice order is live; Rep. Beatty's suit is active; no endowment statute has been rewritten; and no artist-visa or grant-conditioning campaign appears anywhere in the record. The dial scores a captured flagship, not a purged field. What would move it down: the renovation money released without a naming condition, or the building reopened on court order with the name struck. What would move it up: the first grant or visa denial tied to the content of a work.
Gate rule adopted: a candidate list must contain at least one institution whose function is legitimation, not procedure. Without that line, the audit will always return the institutions that issue permissions and never the ones that issue meanings.

5 · Registers — what each pile can and cannot tell you

A forecast is a bet: a claim with a price, a deadline and two possible endings. It is the only pile that can be right or wrong, so it is the only pile that scores.
An accumulation is a tally: no deadline, no resolution, just a count that goes up. Useful as a measurement, useless as a prediction — a tally can never be wrong.
A thesis is a frame: an argument about how the system works. No deadline either, so nothing settles it, so it sits there burning attention while looking unfinished.
RegisterCardsResolvedRateWhat a low rate means
Rate is resolved divided by the cards that can score. A low rate in the forecast register is a result and it costs something. A low rate in the thesis register is an instruction: the claim was never written in a form the world could contradict, so the fix is to rewrite it as a bet or retire it.
The distinction that changes the headline. Twelve cards carry an outcome. Four of them are grandfathered pre-gate cards whose anchors were written after the fact, so they are held out of scoring. Score every resolved card and the forecast rate reads 12 of 58, 21%. Score only the cards that could be scored honestly and it reads 8 of 47, 17%. The second number is the one the board should print; the first is what a careless count produces. A gate rule should enforce the arithmetic rather than trust the reading: resolved && skill_eligible===false must produce a stated quarantine reason, or the push fails.

6 · MGET wire ledger

WIRE LEDGERLoading…
Not dropped. The ledger is the running record of what the wire said against each leg — every entry is a dated observation with a verdict, and it is the only place the model can be caught being wrong on a Tuesday. Collapsed by default because it is 469 entries and grows by roughly ten a day, and grouped by age so an old entry can never pose as a new one: last 7 days, last 30, older. Twelve per group, newest first, with a count on every group header. Full run stays on the live board.
How to read a row. The right-hand stamp is when it was filed. The label on the left is derived from the entry itself, not from the track name — genre is what the wire was carrying (oil, courts, media), subject is what the entry is about, and the NAME chip is a named actor only when the entry names one. The scraped track string is kept in the tooltip, where it cannot be read as a claim. Entries with no time filed show a date alone; an entry with no time is not a claim about the morning.
