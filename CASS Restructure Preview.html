2.2 Micro · what feeds each leg
Four anchors per leg, each scored 0 to 5 on one written scale, and load = (sum of anchors ÷ 4) × 20. An anchor is something you can go and find, not a judgement about how bad things feel. The legs read in model order: M, G, E, T.
The computed column is what the anchors produce; the live board column is the hand-set number it would replace. Where they differ, the anchor reading is the one with a paper trail — and T's gap is the reason this instrument exists.

3 · Institutional coverage — where the dial set has holes

Eight dials is not a list of institutions, it is a list of the ones that happened to be scored. Audited against four admission rules:
RuleTest
R1 · GateDoes it sit astride a decision another actor must clear to complete a transfer of function? A gate is a veto, not an opinion.
R2 · Capture targetWould its compliance decide whether an enforcement action actually lands on the street, or whether a claim can be published at all?
R3 · Not already inside a dialSub-components do not get their own dial. The bar is not the courts; the accreditation office is not the university.
R4 · ScoreableCan a hand-set score be defended at the monthly review from public observables? If you cannot name the observable, it is a mood, not a dial.

The series break nobody would notice

Adding dials changes the mean by arithmetic, not by news. Media at 84, State Governments at 70 and Local Law Enforcement at 74 would take the set from 8 to 11: the mean moves 78 → 78 (627 ÷ 8 = 78.4, 855 ÷ 11 = 77.7 — it rounds back to the same number), while dials at Critical falls from 5 of 8 to 6 of 11, or 63% to 55%. Nothing got calmer. Two Warning dials diluted the share.
Recommendation: store a set version with every reading (8-dial-2026-10 → 11-dial-2026-11) and never compare a mean or a Critical share across a set change. Add the gate rule with the dials: a mean published without a set version is a FAIL. Alternatively admit Media now and hold the other two as watchlist dials, scored on the same scale but excluded from the mean until a dated recalibration — which keeps the series continuous at the cost of a coverage hole that is at least stated.

4 · Registers — what each pile can and cannot tell you

A forecast is a bet: a claim with a price, a deadline and two possible endings. It is the only pile that can be right or wrong, so it is the only pile that scores.
An accumulation is a tally: no deadline, no resolution, just a count that goes up. Useful as a measurement, useless as a prediction — a tally can never be wrong.
A thesis is a frame: an argument about how the system works. No deadline either, so nothing settles it, so it sits there burning attention while looking unfinished.
RegisterCardsResolvedRateWhat a low rate means
Rate is resolved divided by the cards that can score. A low rate in the forecast register is a result and it costs something. A low rate in the thesis register is an instruction: the claim was never written in a form the world could contradict, so the fix is to rewrite it as a bet or retire it.
The distinction that changes the headline. Twelve cards carry an outcome. Four of them are grandfathered pre-gate cards whose anchors were written after the fact, so they are held out of scoring. Score every resolved card and the forecast rate reads 12 of 58, 21%. Score only the cards that could be scored honestly and it reads 8 of 47, 17%. The second number is the one the board should print; the first is what a careless count produces. A gate rule should enforce the arithmetic rather than trust the reading: resolved && skill_eligible===false must produce a stated quarantine reason, or the push fails.
