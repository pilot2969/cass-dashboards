About this page

This is the proposed layout running on real data. Three things differ from the live board. The order changed: state, then flow, then stock, instead of state jumping straight to institutional stress. The registers are separated into forecast, accumulation and thesis, because only one of the three can score. Every model leg now has an instrument — four named anchors per leg, so a leg's pressure reading can be argued with instead of asserted.
The Daily Read is unchanged and keeps second position. Nothing was deleted: thesis cards are still on the board, folded, with their count printed in the header.

2 · Registers — what each pile can and cannot tell you

A forecast is a bet: a claim with a price, a deadline and two possible endings. It is the only pile that can be right or wrong, so it is the only pile that scores.
An accumulation is a tally: no deadline, no resolution, just a count that goes up. Useful as a measurement, useless as a prediction — a tally can never be wrong.
A thesis is a frame: an argument about how the system works. No deadline either, so nothing settles it, so it sits there burning attention while looking unfinished.
RegisterCardsResolvedRateWhat a low rate means
Rate is resolved divided by cards. A low rate in the forecast register is a result and it costs something. A low rate in the thesis register is an instruction: the claim was never written in a form the world could contradict, so the fix is to rewrite it as a bet or retire it.

3 · Leg instrument — what feeds M, G, E and T

Four anchors per leg, each scored 0 to 5 on one written scale, and load = (sum of anchors ÷ 4) × 20. An anchor is something you can go and find, not a judgement about how bad things feel.
The right-hand column is what the anchors compute; the live board column is the hand-set number it would replace. Where they differ, the anchor reading is the one with a paper trail — and T's gap is the reason this instrument exists.
