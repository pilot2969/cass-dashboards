## 1. The bypass is now the target

Houthis claim a drone strike on a pumping station of the Saudi East-West pipeline, a ballistic missile at an oil facility in Yanbu, and an attack on an Aramco building in Riyadh; earlier this week, missile salvos at King Khalid airport and multiple pumping stations on the same line. Riyadh has not commented; the group did not date the attacks.

The East-West line is the bypass that makes a Hormuz closure survivable for Saudi exports. **Attacking the strait and the bypass in the same week removes the alternative rather than the route** — a more durable form of leverage than a blockade, and one that stays below the threshold of state-on-state war because it is claimed by a non-state actor and unconfirmed by the kingdom. Abqaiq 2019 is the operative precedent: half of Saudi output offline in a morning, no retaliation, and every regional actor updating on the fact that infrastructure can be hit and left unanswered.

War-risk premium and Aramco's own capacity language are the two things to watch; escort movements are the third.

## 5. Alaska: capacity as the deciding argument
