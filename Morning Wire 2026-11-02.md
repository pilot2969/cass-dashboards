## Lead: the war now has a start date, and it is the day after the vote

Two things happened this weekend that fit together.

The Doha round broke without agreement. Iranian negotiators left Qatar on Sunday, a draft proposal is circulating, and a follow-up meeting is possible (Reuters). Foreign Minister Araghchi said consultations continue over a "two-day plan" to reopen Hormuz with "no final conclusion", and that a positive outcome depends on how Washington answers Iran's questions. Foreign Ministry spokesman Baghaei added this morning that Tehran does not expect a new US attack after the midterms, and that the delegation did not leave over difficulties.

The other thing is why that reassurance should be discounted. Axios reports that Trump and Netanyahu spoke on Saturday about whether and when to escalate, with Netanyahu pressing for strikes *immediately after* the midterms and Trump "eager to let negotiations play out." An Israeli source called the call "tough"; an American source called it "direct." Trump has said publicly there will be no new attacks before the vote and has not ruled any out afterwards, and he still describes "one or two targets" left to take care of.

Put those together and the calendar does the work that the analysis usually has to. The pre-vote commitment was never a peace commitment; it was a scheduling decision. Tuesday removes the constraint that produced it. Meanwhile the Iranian side is telling its own audience that no attack is coming — which is either a read on American intentions or a message designed to keep the talks alive.

Underneath both, the shipping war has not paused for the negotiations. The Qatari LNG carrier *Al Mayeda* — Pakistan-bound, and cleared for passage by Iran — was struck by unidentified objects in the Gulf on Friday evening. Qatar condemned the attack and reserved the right to take "all necessary measures." UK Maritime Trade Operations reported two projectiles hitting a tanker off eastern Saudi Arabia this morning, and a fuel boat burned near Sirik on Saturday.

## Secondary stack

1. **Oil is now trading the diplomacy, not the attacks.** Brent fell roughly 3% to about $110 and WTI to about $101 this morning — off Friday's four-year high of $116.72 — despite the fresh tanker strikes. OPEC+ held December targets. The tape is being moved by an expectation rather than a flow, which brings us to the claim underneath it.
2. **The Hormuz number is an assertion, not a measurement.** Treasury Secretary Bessent posted on Saturday that Hormuz transit levels are "back to pre-conflict levels minus Iranian barrels" and that "the world can live without Iranian oil." Against that: CENTCOM's own count of redirected vessels stands at 144, independent tracker GEF logged day 242 as CRITICAL with 23 attacks on shipping since 1 October and roughly 492 vessels waiting, and LNG flows through the strait ran at about 30% of pre-war volumes in October. A treasury statement that moves a 3-4% oil tape while the operational picture stays critical is worth flagging as its own phenomenon.
3. **The legal leg of the contestation strategy collapsed in three courts in a week.** Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed the Alaska suit on Sunday, holding the US has no recognised interest in a particular outcome — removing a federal obstacle to certifying a Senate race the administration wanted frozen. The Ninth Circuit denied California relief for a second time on 10-30, 2-1, majority calling the emergency "at least part of the government's own making." No Supreme Court application was confirmed as of this morning, though Democracy Docket reports one is expected. The ~1,000-observer deployment proceeds regardless.
4. **Michigan finished splitting.** Stevens endorsed Rogers; the President said he would "love" for her to join the Republican Party. Two days after the unity event was called off, the state's Democratic coalition is running as two campaigns.
5. **Poles are closing Democratic.** The final NYT/Siena battleground survey has Democrats ahead in five of seven key Senate races with no clear leader in the other two. An NBC poll cited in regional coverage puts disapproval of the President at 60%, with prices tied to the war.
6. **Poland has an external arbiter and no domestic off-ramp.** The Commission said the appointment of eight Tribunal members "lies within the constitutional power of the Sejm," citing a Venice Commission opinion and ECtHR interim measures from 5 May. Tusk ruled out compromise in a televised address; President Nawrocki refused to swear the judges in and called the situation a breach of constitutional order; the justice minister says the Tribunal will resume work in several weeks. Police remain outside the building.
