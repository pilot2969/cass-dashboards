# POLAND — Constitutional Tribunal seizure, 2026-10-31 (13:50 ET)

Sources: Politico EU (10-30, 10-31), Al Jazeera explainer (10-31), AP, Guardian, Le Monde, BBC, Euronews, WaPo.

## What happened, in order

- **Fri afternoon:** Judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska — one of the 15, from the bloc elected by Tusk's parliamentary majority — announced one-month suspensions of tribunal president **Bogdan Święczkowski** and deputy **Bartłomiej Sochański**, and said she was taking over management of the court. Both are PiS appointees.
- **Authority contested at the root:** her claim rests on total length of judicial service, not longest service *on the tribunal*. Święczkowski rejected her authority outright and called it a "coup d'état." Sochański was turned away at the gates.
- **Fri evening:** riot police deployed to the Warsaw building — officially on Korwin-Piotrowska's report of threats to judges and staff. Police say they have not entered; three detained after climbing the fence. Kaczyński joined the protest and tried to force entry. PiS had a protest camp outside for weeks already.
- **Fri night:** President **Karol Nawrocki** turned his plane around mid-flight to Senegal.
- **Just after midnight Sat:** Nawrocki physically pushed through the entrance, past Korwin-Piotrowska, and told the newly elected judges to their faces: "You are not judges." Government side calls it a scuffle; his aide calls the judges "usurpers" who manhandled the president. Video from both sides circulating.
- **~5am Sat:** presidential statement accusing the government of "forcibly seizing a constitutional institution," demanding police leave the grounds, and summoning emergency consultations with parliamentary parties today under a "threat to Poland's internal security" framing.
- **Separately:** Justice Minister/prosecutor general **Waldemar Żurek** filed five immunity-lift requests against Święczkowski and his deputy, backed by 50 volumes of case files — the on-ramp to criminal proceedings.

## Why the ground is this soft

The tribunal has been contested since 2015: three judges validly elected by the outgoing parliament, never sworn in by Duda; three replacements seated anyway. ECtHR (2021) and the CJEU (Dec 2025) both held the body not an independent tribunal — which is the legal footing Tusk's government has used to stop publishing its rulings. This year parliament elected eight judges to the 15-seat bench; Nawrocki swore in three and withheld the oath from five; those five took oaths in the Sejm instead, and Święczkowski barred them from adjudicating.

So the current move is not one side breaking a settled rule. It is two competing theories of legitimacy — parliamentary election versus presidential oath — each with a plausible pedigree, executed through physical control of a building.

## Signals that matter more than the noise

1. **Nov 3 tribunal judges' assembly.** Called to consider lifting immunities. If the assembly's composition or quorum is challenged, the confrontation moves from a leadership dispute to a nullification fight — every ruling either camp issues becomes non-reviewable by the other.
2. **Whether police stay.** Nawrocki demanded they leave; if they do, Korwin-Piotrowska's authority visibly collapses. If they don't, the "police in a constitutional court" precedent becomes the durable fact — Matczak's warning, and the one that outlives this government.
3. **Whether the immunity referrals proceed.** Criminal process against a sitting tribunal president converts a political crisis into a personal one, much harder to unwind.
4. **EU posture.** Brussels has the CJEU ruling in hand and a strong incentive to stay quiet — anything it says gets read as taking a side in a member state's internal fight three weeks before its own budget negotiation round.
5. **The 2027 election clock.** Tusk's coalition is thin (Pełczyńska-Nałęcz left the government and Polska 2050's leadership on Oct 22), Kraków's mayoral race went to an independent against his candidate. This reads as base-mobilization over a stalled reform record — the risk Matczak flags is alienating the moderates he needs.

## Read

The regime-signature to watch is not the police deployment; it's the **claim of emergency**. Nawrocki has already hit the "threat to internal security" register, which is the standard door to extraordinary procedure. Everything else — the scuffle at the door, the coup language from both camps, the immunity filings — is downstream of who gets to say the word "emergency" and have it stick.

## Contrast worth holding

Poland's fight is over *which* institution is legitimate. The US fight this week is over whether *any* institution's output will be accepted. Different failure modes: Poland risks a captured court with a rival court beside it; the US risks a contested count with no referee. Both produce rulings nobody can enforce — which is the actual end state to price.
