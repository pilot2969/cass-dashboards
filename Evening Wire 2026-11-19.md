## Lead: the count fight ends in Alaska and the apparatus fight starts in Washington

**The facts.** The administration moved to have its own lawsuit dismissed in the challenge to Alaska's US Senate race (AP, 5 outlets). In the same window, Trump's pick to lead the federal agency that protects US elections said January 6 was not an "attack" (AP, 3 outlets).

**Why these two belong together.** Read alone, the first is a retreat and the second is a staffing story. Read together they are one instrument changing address. Contesting a count requires a state official to act unlawfully: Alaska ran that channel three times in five days — a preemptive suit filed 22 October and dismissed 1 November, roughly twenty criminal target letters on election day, then a presidential demand on 5 November that the totals be "immediately given to the REAL Dan Sullivan" — and produced no carrier. Appointing the person who administers elections requires only a nomination: lawful, permanent, and it does not need anyone to break a rule.

That is the same trade recorded in last night's specimen. The 11 November White House counsel letter threatened an expensive instrument (a criminal referral against members of Congress); the 19 November position withholds a free one, because non-compliance with a document request is the executive's default and the remedy runs through a court whose clock the administration can run out. When the costly channel fails, the move is not to a louder version of it. It is to the channel whose default state is already the desired state.

**On the Alaska card.** `alaska-transfer-demand-institutional-carrier` moves 35 → 30, not flipped. Its falsifier is a named endorsement, not litigation, so a dismissal does not resolve it — silence to 31 December is still the test and 41 days remain. But the dismissal removes the road a carrier would have travelled down, and fifteen days have passed with no named national Republican endorsing the transfer itself. Narrow it in the tracking note instead of the score: what remains possible by year end is an endorsement in the absence of any live proceeding, which is a weaker object than the card was written to catch.

**What the dismissal is and is not.** It is not a retreat from a position. The suit was the president's own — "I'm the one that said do it" — and it was going to lose. A voluntary dismissal before an adverse ruling is an exit at the cost of one filing, and it leaves the underlying proposition unadjudicated, which is the point. Losing a case establishes a fact. Withdrawing one establishes nothing, and nothing is the more useful of the two here.
