## Lead: the war has been rescheduled, not cancelled — and both sides have now said what happens if the talks fail

Yesterday the administration's public line moved in two directions at once. Rubio said Saturday that Trump has not decided whether to strike Iran and would prefer a diplomatic resolution. The same day, Trump was reported saying the US needs to "take care of one or two sites," and to have spoken with Netanyahu about renewed strikes. The White House's pre-vote commitment — no attack before the election — still looks held, which means the operative question is no longer *whether* a strike is being prepared but *what would stop one*.

Into that gap, Tehran put its own escalation marker. Security Council chief Mohsen Rezaei told state media Iran is debating whether to keep its longstanding ban on nuclear weapons if hostilities resume — with no decision taken. That is not a doctrine change. It is the price signal a state publishes when it wants the other side's strike planners to price in a nuclear horizon. It also hands the strike advocates in Washington exactly the argument they have lacked: that delay degrades.

And the talks are still running. Qatari mediators met Iranian envoys in Doha again this weekend, working the seven-day step-by-step framework carried to Tehran a week ago. Doha is now the only process standing between the two clocks.

Read the sequence rather than the quotes. On 10-31 we noted the sequencing promise as the cleanest falsifier on the board: no strike before the vote. Two days out, that promise is intact and the war is intact with it — the strike package publicly promised to the Gulf is still waiting on the Roosevelt's arrival in theater, which buys everyone another few weeks of calendar. What has changed is that the negotiation is no longer being described by Washington as a way to avoid war. It is being described as one option among two.

## Secondary stack

1. **The European Commission picked a side in Warsaw, and it is the parliament's.** A Commission spokesperson said today that appointing the eight new Tribunal members "lies within the constitutional power of the Sejm," and pointed to the ECtHR's interim measures of 5 May 2026 requiring Polish authorities to refrain from hindering the Sejm-appointed judges in taking up their duties. Tusk has said he will not compromise. This matters more than the street theatre: the crisis was, as of Friday, two competing theories of legitimacy with no external arbiter. It now has one — and the arbiter is the same ECtHR and Commission record that the government has been using to stop publishing Tribunal rulings. The president's position is reduced to the oath and the building. Watch the Nov 3 judges' assembly on the immunity requests: composition, not outcome, is the tell.

2. **Michigan's unity event is off before it happened.** El-Sayed's campaign asked Haley Stevens to stay away from today's Detroit church event; ABC7 now reports the two will not campaign together at all. Dan Sullivan, meanwhile, said he will accept the Alaska result while calling the Peltola ballot scheme a display of "disdain" for voters. Note the asymmetry: the Republican in a contested race pre-accepts the count, the Democratic tent in the marquee Senate race cannot hold a photo line two days out. Neither is a polling datum; both are turnout-machine data.

3. **DOJ has still not gone to the Supreme Court on California.** As of this morning there is no application on the docket; the reporting remains "setting up a potential showdown." The relevant precedent is already there: in *Trump v. California* (No. 26A124, per curiam, 24 August 2026) the Court stayed an injunction against the election-integrity executive order on the government's standing argument, without reaching the merits. Any filing this weekend inherits that posture — and a stay granted Monday would be a ruling that never has to be enforced. That is the whole game now: not the merits, the clock.

4. **Trump closes in Iowa and Kansas, on nostalgia.** Near-daily rallies, greatest-hits set, in states that were never in question — read as a farewell tour rather than a turnout operation. The actual marginal-dollar races are elsewhere and quieter, which is what a campaign does when it believes the base is not the problem.
