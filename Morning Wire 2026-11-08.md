## Lead: the circle shrank, and the enforcer inherited it

On Friday afternoon the president removed Susie Wiles as chief of staff — the longest-tenured holder of that job in the term — and named **Stephen Miller** in her place. Miller was already deputy chief of staff and homeland security adviser, and he is the architect of the mass-deportation programme. The change came three days after Republicans lost both chambers of Congress.

The obvious read is chaos: a losing administration reshuffles. That is the wrong read, and it is wrong in a way our own instrument would have ratified.

**Leg 2 counts elite exits.** Nobody exited. The aggregate turnover number barely moves this week. What moved is the *centre of gravity* of the decision process: it contracted, and it contracted toward the portfolio with the least legal friction. Counting departures would have logged this week as stability. It was consolidation — and consolidation after a defeat is the opposite of the moderation a defeat is supposed to produce.

The historical shape is consistent enough to be a pattern rather than an anecdote. After the NSDAP lost 34 seats in November 1932, the party did not moderate: Strasser resigned on 8 December, the internal alternative was eliminated, authority concentrated in the leader's office, and the chancellorship arrived eight weeks later. Nixon's answer to Watergate was to contract the circle to the enforcers and then fire the prosecutors; that held for a year before the removal mechanism completed. The general form: **when the coalition shrinks, the circle shrinks faster than the coalition, and the enforcers inherit the residual authority.**

What would disconfirm it: moderation from the enforcer. Three observables. Any departure of the White House counsel, the legislative affairs director or the OMB director within thirty days would be further narrowing. Immigration enforcement tempo rising without new statutory authority would be acceleration. The Iran decision reverting to a wide principals' process would be restraint, and if that happens the reading is wrong and gets filed as wrong.

## 1. Iran: the war stopped announcing itself

Blasts were heard Saturday evening at **Qeshm, Sirik, Bandar Abbas and Jask**. Tasnim reported two missiles hit Qeshm. Iran's state television then said officials had **not confirmed any missile strikes on Iranian territory** and suggested the explosions were related to *Iranian military activity* in the waterway. Trump, speaking in West Palm Beach on Friday, confirmed US strikes on Iranian air-defence equipment and called them **"minor"**: "they built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five." No large-scale operation has appeared.

At sea, both sides report damage and neither accepts the other's account. The IRGC Navy says two tankers hit mines and a third caught fire in Hormuz on an "unauthorised route" (Fars); the US says its aircraft disabled a vessel's stern in the Gulf of Oman beyond the blockade; UKMTO logged an incident 18nm east of Khor Fakkan with damage unknown. Reuters could not confirm any of it. Separately, Saudi Arabia's energy ministry said an **Aramco administrative building in Riyadh** was damaged by falling debris after an attempted attack — no casualties — as the Houthi–Saudi track escalates on its own clock.

**The structure of the claims is the story, not the casualty count.** Within four days the war moved from a decision to be announced ("destroy the rest of the country") to attrition that each side describes as something other than the other side's attack. Ambiguity is doing work: it lets both parties keep striking while denying that a decision has been taken — exactly what a principal who has publicly removed his own ceiling needs. The 2019 Gulf shipping attacks and Abqaiq–Khurais ran this way; so did the Tanker War of 1987–88, at much higher volume.

**And this is first an instrument problem, which I should name before a postmortem does.** Our escalation index reads visible packages — air picture, tanker streams, named strike waves. If the marginal unit of this war becomes the unattributable ship incident, the index will report restraint while attrition rises. It needs a second channel that does not depend on either side's story: **insured hull losses and Gulf war-risk premia**, priced by people with money at stake. A rising premium against a flat air picture is the signature of escalation below the visible threshold.

## 2. Kraków: nine dead, and the difference between a movement and a man

A gunman drove into Kraków's Main Square on Saturday afternoon and opened fire from a car with an AR-15-style rifle — **six dead** (four women, two men, one a British citizen), sixteen injured. Police shot and wounded him. Counter-terrorism officers then found **three more bodies** at his home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, reported as family members. Poland's defence minister called it an act of terror; authorities describe the suspect as a Polish man with **ultranationalist, far-right views**. National mourning was declared for today.

I am scoring this **neutral**, deliberately. Our instruments measure movements — capacity, coordination, elite uptake. This is one individual with an ideology and no discernible organisation, and the death toll says nothing about the strength of the milieu he drew from. Logging it as movement capacity would repeat the error of reading a lone hacker as state capability. What it establishes is *availability*: the vocabulary is in circulation, and one person can act on it without a network.

The observable is the state's response, because Poland is already contesting the legitimacy of its own judicial institutions. Two paths: the act is processed as **crime**, ideology as motive — the Norwegian path after 2011, where 77 deaths produced no crackdown on the milieu and one trial; or processed as **security**, with emergency legislation, expanded service powers and a movement category — closer to the German response after Halle and Hanau, which produced gun-law and domestic-intelligence changes within a year. The second path is cheaper to justify in a state whose ordinary machinery is already being called illegitimate. That is the docking test, not the shooting.
