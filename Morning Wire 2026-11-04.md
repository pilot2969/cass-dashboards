## Lead: the republicans lost both chambers, and the country lost a bit of its settled map

Democrats won the House and the Senate. NBC's estimate puts the House at 258 seats and rising, 19+ net gains — against a redistricting map that the pre-registered House card named, in advance, as the single thing capable of eating a 2018-sized national lead. It did not bite. New York Times reporting has flips in rural North Carolina, central Kentucky, the St. Louis and Omaha suburbs, and all along the Texas border.

The Senate: majority at 54, seven Republican seats flipped — North Carolina (Roy Cooper, first Democratic Senate win there since 2008), South Carolina (Annie Andrews), Ohio (Sherrod Brown over appointed incumbent Jon Husted in the special, two years of the term), Texas (James Talarico, first Democratic senator from the state in decades), Kansas (Adam Hamilton), Iowa (Josh Turek), Maine (Troy Jackson). Abdul El-Sayed wins Michigan and becomes the first Muslim senator in US history.

Governors: Democratic majority for the first time in 16 years, four offices flipped, Ohio among them — Mike Acton over the Republican, roughly 50.5–46.1.

The composition question is settled, and not in the direction the doomsayers were running. NBC's early read has more than half of voters disapproving of the president's job performance in Alaska and in five of the six red states Democrats flipped. This was defection, not a base-turnout substitution — the wave came from people who had been voting Republican and stopped.
