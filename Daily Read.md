<summary><h2>DOJ charges 28 in California — the vote-fraud story gets its cast</h2></summary>

<h3>What happened</h3>
<p>The Justice Department charged 28 green card holders in California with illegally registering to vote or voting, and is separately suing the state to block roughly 20,000 ballots. The AP has also fact-checked the Census Bureau report the White House has been citing: it does not show the mass noncitizen voting the President claims happened in 2020.</p>

<h3>Why it matters</h3>
<p>Prosecutions of this kind — filed two weeks before an election — are how a narrative acquires names, faces and headlines. Whatever the merits of any single case, the aggregate effect is to pre-stock an election story.</p>

<h3>The pattern</h3>
<p>The election-fraud argument has been built in stages: "the polls are fake" (since late September), "early voting is skewed" (mid-October), and now exhibits. An accusation that must be ready <i>before</i> the votes are counted.</p>

<h3>What history says</h3>
<p>1930s-era emergency politics rarely invented grievances in the moment; it assembled them from artifacts manufactured quietly in advance. California's suit is under court watch — the first ruling in that case is the legal tell.</p>

<h3>What we're watching next</h3>
<p>A court ruling on the ballot-blocking suit; whether DOJ expands to more states; whether the language shifts from "fake polls" to "fake count" once votes are actually being tallied.</p>

<summary><h2>Trump shifts on Ukraine as Europe's streets burn over prices</h2></summary>

<h3>What happened</h3>
<p>US ties to Ukraine hit a new low after a policy shift, while the administration courts Moscow (Witkoff to Moscow; a Trump meeting with Putin and Xi in China is reported) and the press secretary quit after less than a week. Across the Atlantic, fuel, food and housing protests set streets ablaze from France to Albania.</p>

<h3>Why it matters</h3>
<p>Restored Starshield intelligence sharing was openly framed as a reward for halting strikes on Russian refineries — alignment with Moscow is being purchased in the same currency. And the European riots are the physical version of a price signal routed as "someone's fault": when inflation is blamed on enemies rather than managed, the street is the reprice.</p>

<h3>The pattern</h3>
<p>Same grid as the domestic 3.7% CPI story routed to a predecessor. Price pain needs a villain; the villain is chosen before the data is published.</p>

<h3>What history says</h3>
<p>Weimar's hyperinflation cycle taught that crowds follow whoever names the enemy first. France 2026 and Ohio 2026 are running the same play at different speeds.</p>

<h3>What we're watching next</h3>
<p>Witkoff's Moscow return and what it trades; whether European unrest mutates into anti-NATO framing; the next US scapegoat attribution before Nov 3.</p>
