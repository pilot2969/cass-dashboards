<summary>[09:35] DOJ charges 28 in California — the vote-fraud story gets its cast</summary>

**What happened.** The Justice Department charged 28 green card holders in California with illegally registering to vote or voting, and is separately suing the state to block roughly 20,000 ballots. The AP has fact-checked the Census Bureau report the White House keeps citing: it does not show the mass noncitizen voting the President claims happened in 2020.

**Why it matters.** Prosecutions filed two weeks before an election are how a narrative acquires names, faces and headlines. Whatever the merits of any single case, the aggregate effect is to pre-stock an election story.

**The pattern.** The election-fraud argument has been built in stages: "the polls are fake" (late September), "early voting is skewed" (mid-October), and now exhibits. An accusation that must be ready *before* the votes are counted.

**What history says.** Emergency politics rarely invents grievances in the moment; it assembles them from artifacts manufactured quietly in advance. The California suit is under court watch — the first ruling is the legal tell.

**Watching next.** A ruling on the ballot-blocking suit; whether DOJ expands to more states; whether the language shifts from "fake polls" to "fake count" once votes are being tallied.
