## Lead: the fix caused the failure, and the bill went from thirty to fifty

The aviation system came back overnight — travel was nearing normal by early Monday, which is the one part of this that closed on schedule. Everything else opened.

**The explanation moved again, and this time it moved toward the cause.** Transportation Secretary Duffy told CNN that the meltdown was triggered by an operation to change obsolete copper wire, which "apparently resulted in floods of data overwhelming the wider network." Set that against Saturday's account: an unforeseeable contractor maintenance mistake at a remote location. The new account says the router work itself produced the failure — the modernisation was the fault, not an accident that interrupted it. That is a materially different claim, offered by the same official, in a Sunday-studio setting rather than a press conference.

**The money moved first and justified later.** Duffy raised the request from $30 billion to $50 billion with no detail on what the additional $20 billion buys, and toured the Sunday shows asking Congress to act. Van Hollen, who is challenging Schumer for the Senate leadership, answered with the number that will define this fight: Congress already appropriated $12 billion in the President's domestic package, and about a third has been spent. Himes added the staffing half — 1,300 FAA employees retired or resigned and 400 probationary staff were let go under the Department of Government Efficiency, numbers he attributed to Musk's purge. Neither of those is a claim about hardware.

**The audit finding is the one that will outlast the outage.** The GAO warned in September (GAO-26-107992) that the FAA, while making progress, "could be prioritizing speed over costs and air traffic disruptions," and that it lacked a lifecycle cost estimate or a master schedule for the modernisation projects. That is a federal auditor saying the agency cannot currently say what the programme costs or when it lands. A fifty-billion-dollar request arriving without a lifecycle estimate, six weeks after the auditor flagged the absence, is the programme's real problem — not the politics of the vote.

**And the timing is locked.** The lame-duck Congress has to fund the government next month, Thanksgiving travel starts in nine days, and the oversight questions now arrive from a chamber whose majority changes on 3 January. Any appropriation made in the next seven weeks is made by the outgoing majority and audited by the incoming one. That is the strongest position from which to ask for money and the weakest from which to spend it.

**One provenance caution.** AP reports questions circling the FAA's new SMART AI system and, in the same piece, that officials say there is no link to the ground stop. Both halves are doing work: an AI system acquiring a public association with a national outage, in the absence of any stated mechanism. Record it as a question attached to a system, not a finding about one.

## 1. Port Shuaiba: the survivor's complaint, and an investigation nobody has read

This is the most consequential document of the weekend and it entered the corpus nowhere until this run.

Maj. Stephen Ramsbottom, wounded in the 1 March drone attack on a tactical operations centre at Port Shuaiba in Kuwait, filed a whistleblower complaint on 13 November with the House and Senate Armed Services committees. CBS reported it Sunday; TIME has the detail this morning. The attack came a day after the US and Israel opened the war on 28 February: six soldiers killed, more than thirty wounded, the deadliest single attack on American troops since 2021. Twenty-three US service members have died across eight months.

The content is the part that matters. Ramsbottom says he raised concerns about the absence of protection from aerial attack twice and was told: "We don't have to worry about protection." A counter-drone system "never arrived at Shuaiba and we don't know if it was ever even ordered to or requested." The post had "no physician, no fixed aid station, and only one ambulance." Roughly seventy soldiers of the 103rd Sustainment Command had been moved there from Camp Arifjan about a week before the war began — from a major base to a smaller outpost inside Iranian missile range. The Washington Post reported in July that commanders had ignored intelligence warnings the site was a probable target.

**The gap is documentary, not rhetorical.** The Pentagon announced an investigation on 3 March; AP reported it concluded in June; families were briefed privately in July; the findings have never been published. A widow told CBS officials acknowledged no mistakes. Hegseth's account on 2 March — a "squirter" that got through, hitting a centre that "was fortified" — is now contradicted by the man who was inside it. Spokesman Parnell's "six-foot walls" is a claim about mortars; Ramsbottom's is about drones. Those are different threats and only one of them was designed for.

**Why it lands now.** Blumenthal, ranking member on the Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, sent a letter Sunday night demanding a classified briefing with Hegseth and "all force protection assessments" for the post. Warren and Ryan called for public hearings in September. Democrats take both chambers in January and have already scheduled war-oversight investigations. The Hegseth impeachment vehicle died in a referral vote on 10 November; this is a different instrument pointed at the same person, and it arrives with a named complainant, a filed document and a committee request. That is the shape that survives a change of session.

## 2. Iran: two fronts, one weekend, and the provocation the President asked not to receive

Iran fired drones at US bases in Bahrain on Sunday — the IRGC claimed six against a base, Bahrain said its air defences intercepted and destroyed several, the first interceptions by the kingdom since September. Two days earlier the Guards claimed a strike on a US early-warning radar in Bahrain; the US military denied it and said two drones were intercepted with no damage. Crude is moving at near pre-war volumes, but the Guards also claimed two tankers were on fire after striking mines in Hormuz, unverified. Foreign Minister Araqchi goes to Oman on Tuesday.

The second front is the one with measurable consequences. Saudi-backed government forces announced a redeployment away from the Bab el-Mandeb to defend Aden — the effective end of a six-week counteroffensive to clear the Houthis from the mouth of the Red Sea. The Houthis claimed attacks on King Khalid International Airport, a financial centre in Riyadh and two pumping stations on the East-West pipeline. Saudi Arabia has been rerouting about four million barrels a day through that pipeline to Yanbu: four per cent of global supply, now a declared target. Taiz is besieged, roughly half of the country's 300,000 newly displaced are there, and aid cannot get in.

**Read it against the President's own condition.** He said in recent days he might hold off attacking Iran if there were no new provocations from Tehran or the Houthis. Sunday supplied several. That does not fire the escalation node — the threshold requires a named operational phase, or a mass salvo with confirmed effect, and there is still not one confirmed hit — but it removes the stated restraint and hands the Gulf states a grievance they can use in either direction.

## 3. Alaska: a Democrat, and the counting rule that produced one

Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, 37, a former state legislator, has won the Alaska governorship, defeating three Republicans including the President's endorsed candidate, Bernadette Wilson. First Democratic governor of the state in nearly three decades. Democrats now hold 30 governorships.

The mechanism is ranked-choice voting plus a split opposition: with three Republicans dividing the first-preference vote, the transfer rounds produced a Democratic majority that no head-to-head contest would have. That is the same instrument the corpus has been tracking as a live transfer-demand question, and the result settles it in the least convenient way for the demand — the count ran, produced a winner, and the winner was the other party's. Whether the demand now acquires an institutional carrier (a legislator, a committee, a lawsuit) is the still-open half of that card, and it is not decidable today.

**Do not read this as a national signal.** Alaska's result was called five days after the wave and adds a governorship to a total already counted; the informative content is procedural, not electoral. The one thing worth carrying: a voting rule that was designed to reward consensus produced a partisan flip because the field was split — which is a fact about incentives, not about Alaska's electorate.

## 4. California: a court-appointed examiner, and a recirculation flagged as news

The state-programme sweep returned seven California election-administration hits this morning, led by "judge to appoint special master in California voting case in setback to DOJ's attempt to quickly intervene." Dropping a case you have already lost the first round of is not the same as conceding the claim, and the master's evaluation continues. ## 5. Shenzhen: the meeting acquired a subject

China's foreign ministry said Monday that Thursday's trilateral between Xi, Trump and Putin in Shenzhen is of "groundbreaking significance" and will "safeguard world peace" and promote global stability. That is the first named agenda for a meeting that on Sunday had none — no format, no communiqué, and a host that had spent a fortnight declining to host an arms-control conversation at all.

"World peace" is a category, not a document. Beijing naming the theme does two things: it converts a photograph into a summit with declared deliverables, and it sets the frame in which any outcome will be judged. Meanwhile Reuters reports the US and China are working on an AI incident channel — a bilateral technical instrument between two of the three principals, which is precisely the kind of thing that gets announced around a summit rather than at one.

**What to hold.** The test is unchanged from Sunday: whether anything emerges with inspectors, verification language or a signed text. A stated theme is not that. But a reluctant host that names the theme has already conceded the meeting is his, and that is a shift in kind.
