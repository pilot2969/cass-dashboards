## Lead: the planes are still not flying, and the explanation has split in two

College football moved with it: the SEC pushed No. 19 Ole Miss–Oklahoma to Sunday, and the Sooners' athletic director publicly attacked the decision. The stop was lifted at 20:28 ET Friday. The recovery is the story on every front page and it is the least interesting part of the file.

**The interesting part is that two incompatible explanations are now running in the same corpus, both from respectable places.**

The first is the one the podium gave Friday night: new equipment overwhelmed an older system, switches and routers returned inconsistent data, the agency shut it down. The second arrived overnight and is the day's most-carried story — nine outlets, thirty-seven items — a Reuters examination reporting that **old air traffic control equipment regularly disrupts US flights**. Not an update gone wrong. A chronic condition, described as routine, in the same week the agency says a change to its network took the national airspace down for four hours.

These are not the same claim and neither has been retracted. If the chronic-equipment account is right, Friday was not an anomaly but a threshold crossed, and the "our switches got overloaded with data" account is a story told about a failure whose cause nobody had established yet. If the podium account is right, Reuters has described a background condition and mislabelled it as the cause. **Both readings produce the same policy demand**, which is why the ambiguity will not be resolved by anyone who wants the money.

## 4. America has lost its measles-elimination status

The United States has lost measles-elimination status after 26 years, per WSJ, carried this morning by Reuters and DW. Elimination status is a formal designation: it means continuous transmission of a disease that was declared gone from the country has lasted more than twelve months. It is not a metaphor and it is not reversible by announcement.

**This is the fourth item in a series this wire has been keeping without a name.** CDC eliminated $29m in HIV prevention funding for Texas (reported 11-13). The administration is diverting billions in global health funding away from low-income countries, against Congress's stated plans (CNN this morning). State health capacity has been reduced in three states in a fortnight. Now a formal status the country had held since 2000 is gone.

**The pattern is subtraction, and subtraction does not produce events, which is why it never leads a wire.** Nothing happens on the day a designation lapses. The measurable consequence arrives years later, in outbreak counts, in a population that has never seen the disease, in a vaccination rate that takes a decade to rebuild. Against the rest of this morning's tape — a ground stop, a called-off strike, a governor's race — the loss of elimination status is the item with the longest half-life and the least coverage.

Related, and this morning: Pope Leo XIV is in Peru, drawing crowds in the hundreds of thousands, and the coverage notes low vaccination rates in the region alongside it. The two facts sit in the same paragraph in three feeds and are not connected by any of them.
