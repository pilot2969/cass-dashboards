## Lead: the contractor has a name, and the answer arrived in a day

At 11:20 this morning the file's finding was that two incompatible explanations for Friday's four-hour ground stop were running side by side, neither retracted, both arguing for the same $50bn. By Saturday afternoon that split had closed — and it closed the way the E leg is supposed to resolve, with an owner named.

Sean Duffy attributed the failure to maintenance by **L3Harris**, a long-standing federal contractor, converting a copper line to fibre at what he called a remote location. Bryan Bedford, the FAA administrator, said a single router began flooding the network, cutting communications and radar; the agency has disabled that function on the roughly sixty routers of the same type in service nationwide and is testing with the contractor. Bedford twice ruled out a cyberattack and any AI involvement: "zero evidence of anything nefarious, and zero evidence of anything AI-related." L3Harris issued two sentences taking the matter seriously and nothing else. The tally: ~8,200 cancellations, over 31% of Friday's schedule (Cirium), normalisation not before Sunday.

**What to notice is the mechanism of resolution, not the resolution.** The cause was not established by a document. It was established by a secretary at a podium naming a company. No IG referral has been announced, no contract action, no change record published. An attribution from a podium is a claim with an author who needs money, and it arrived within fourteen hours of the event. Two things follow. First, the ownership question is now answerable and answerable to one party's account. Second — and this is the reason to keep the leg open rather than close it — naming a vendor is the most convenient available answer to a failure that needs an appropriation, because it closes the question of cause in public while leaving liability open in private, and it lets the argument proceed straight to the cheque.

The political fight did not dissolve with the cause. Duffy went from "billions" to **fifty billion** from the same podium. Stanton said there is bipartisan support for modernisation and balked at the number. Duckworth: "You've literally controlled Congress for the past two years."

**Test status — and this morning's forward signal was answered in part.** T1 called for a hearing noticed by Monday 11-16. Bedford said Saturday he expects "a very transparent hearing." No date, no committee, no notice. **Promised is not noticed**, and the distinction is the whole of the T1 discipline: a hearing that exists only in a press conference is a second announcement, not a papering. T4 (repeat systems failure) remains not hit. The registration stands.

## 1. Iran: I moved the card the wrong way this morning, and I am moving it back

The morning wire read the Reuters pause as the day's signal and dropped the Iran escalation card 18 → 14. The day's decisive fact arrived afterwards and points the other way. Recording the reversal rather than quietly editing the number.

**Axios (Ravid, three sources):** Mossad director **Roman Gofman** — one of Netanyahu's closest confidants — was in Washington Friday meeting CIA director Ratcliffe, then expected in Miami to see Witkoff. Mossad, CIA and the White House all declined comment. The purpose is the inverse of a de-escalation visit: **Netanyahu is pressing the President to resume the war.** Trump has refused — they discussed it on 5 November and he has sided with Riyadh, Doha and Abu Dhabi on blockade-plus-economic-pressure. His own words Thursday: "Saudi Arabia has asked me to please just let it be the way it is now for a while, because [Iran is] disintegrating. Qatar, Kuwait, UAE, Bahrain, all of them would much prefer the waiting period."

**Why the vector moved is structural, not rhetorical.** Netanyahu lost his majority on 27 October. Eisenkot holds the mandate. If Eisenkot forms a government, Netanyahu is out. Israeli opposition figures tell Axios they fear he will attack Iran — **or provoke Iran into attacking Israel** — to break Eisenkot's coalition. Eisenkot says he told the prime minister he "will not allow a lack of oversight" of his actions during the transition, and a source close to him says the situation does not demand immediate military action. On Friday the two discussed resuming attacks.

Around that: the security cabinet ordered strike preparations stepped up on Wednesday; the IDF says preparations for renewed fighting are complete; US military aircraft are arriving in Israel; the US military has reportedly told Israel and the Gulf to prepare for another war — while the President says the war is effectively over. Iranian state media reported two tankers exploding on mines near Hormuz; the IRGC claimed destruction of a US early-warning radar in Bahrain, which **CENTCOM denied**; Iran's deputy UN ambassador told the Security Council Iran "has never closed the door to diplomacy" and would answer bombing with an "immediate and decisive response."

**Threshold precision matters for scoring and I am keeping it.** The card requires a US-announced operational phase or expanded US war aims. An Israeli strike does not satisfy it — and an Israeli strike launched to save a caretaker prime minister would run *against* the card's mechanism, because it would be escalation the United States is actively trying to prevent. So this is a live escalation vector with the wrong principal: it raises the chance the US is pulled in without raising the chance the US initiates. **14 → 26. Not flipped.** Falsifier untouched.

## 2. Strategic: the trilateral is real, and still has no inspectors in it

White House and Kremlin both confirmed: **Trump, Xi and Putin meet Thursday on the APEC sidelines in Shenzhen** on nuclear non-proliferation. First time the heads of the three largest arsenals sit in one room. Yesterday's entry recorded no venue, no format, no preconditions, and neither counterparty confirmed. Venue, date and both principals are now on the record. That is genuine movement and is logged as such.

What has not moved: no account from any of the three governments mentions inspections, telemetry, data exchange or a monitoring body, and the one arrangement that supplies any of it — New START data-sharing — has lapsed. Reykjavík failed on the word *verification*. SALT I took three years and leaned on satellites both sides already had. A first-ever trilateral is a large event; on this record it is not an instrument. The absence of verification language in a meeting this anticipated is not an oversight, it is a decision about what the meeting is for: a meeting that produces numbers can be claimed, a meeting that produces inspections can be failed.

## 4. Subtraction series, fourth entry: the status was revoked and the response was pre-declared unchanged

**PAHO revoked US measles-elimination status** — 26 years, ended because transmission ran continuously past twelve months. 5,412 cases, 590+ hospitalisations, six deaths on CDC's count and ten on state reporting after the agency changed how it counts death reports. Pennsylvania leads. The confirming fact is not the decision but the sentence before it: the CDC director said in November the agency **would not fundamentally change its outbreak response** if the status were lost.

The US has never lost this status before; it came close in 2019 with ~1,300 cases and held. Brazil lost it in 2019, regained it 2024. Venezuela lost 2018, reverified 2023. **Canada lost its designation in 2025 and declared its multi-jurisdictional outbreak over in October of this year.** Restoration is possible — every instance involved an intensified campaign and rebuilt surveillance, run by a government that treated the loss as an emergency. That is the variable, not capability.

Enclosure by abandonment rather than seizure: the state does not take the capability away, it declines to use it, and because nothing happens on the day, the absence is unfalsifiable. Fourth consecutive subtraction item, least coverage of anything in the file.
