## Lead: the exchange went direct, and the planning went joint

Two facts landed within four hours of each other this evening and they point in different directions on the same question.

**Iran claimed a strike on a US base.** At 05:22 GMT the IRGC announced on Telegram that six drones of its aerospace force had targeted "the US air defence radar system at the Al-Riffa helicopter base" in Bahrain. Sirens sounded across the kingdom; the Interior Ministry told residents to shelter; the Bahrain Defence Force says its air defences "intercepted and thwarted several hostile Iranian air attacks" before they reached their targets. The US Embassy in Bahrain issued a heightened-vigilance alert citing "Iranian efforts to target US government officials and facilities in the region." This is the second Iranian claim of a strike on a US radar in three days — the US military rejected the first, saying it intercepted two drones with no damage.

**Read it on the threshold, not the headlines.** Node 2C requires Iran to open a *sustained campaign* — a mass salvo on a city or US base, sustained strait closure, or a strike killing US servicemembers. Six intercepted drones on a radar do not meet that, and no ordnance is confirmed to have landed. What changed is the preceding: the same regime is now claiming, on the record, that it is attacking American installations. Two claims in three days is a pattern even where each individual claim is intercepted. A third claim with confirmed effect fires the node.

**The more consequential reporting is the planning.** The New York Times reports that US and Israeli military officials have drawn up plans for renewed strikes that would likely target **energy infrastructure and other facilities vital to Iran's economy** — and that it is unclear whether another attack could force concessions. Separately, ABC reports a **third carrier strike group, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, entered CENTCOM's area of operations today**, joining the Bush and Washington; more than twenty US Navy ships are enforcing the blockade of Iranian ports.

**So escalation odds rose and the solo-Israeli branch fell.** That is not a contradiction. If American officers are drawing up the target list, the branch in which Israel strikes without US participation loses its distinctiveness, which is why the tree's solo branch is cut tonight even as Node 1 rises. This is the reason the tree was built: a single qualified card could not have held both moves, and would have had to choose which fact to ignore.

**The rest of the night, briefly.** Gofman returned to Israel and briefed the security cabinet, which could authorise an operation "designated either as a military operation or a war" (Ynet). Israel Hayom reports the Mossad passed the CIA new intelligence on an alleged Iranian plan to target Trump and senior officials with explosive-laden drones, corroborated by a second Western service. Gulf states — Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan, Egypt — condemned the Bahrain attack in unusually blunt terms, Riyadh calling it "a violation of international law and the principles of good neighbourliness." That matters: the Gulf is the constituency Trump has been siding with against Netanyahu, and Iran has just handed them a grievance.

**Yemen is the quieter collapse.** Houthi forces claimed ballistic missile and drone attacks on Riyadh, including King Khalid International Airport and pumping stations on the East-West pipeline; the Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted two ballistic missiles and two drones. Near Aden, a Houthi missile killed five at a police checkpoint. And government forces backed by Riyadh **withdrew from the Bab el-Mandeb area, handing the Houthis the chokepoint** — a double squeeze with Hormuz that isolates Gulf producers from both exits.

## 2. Aviation: the explanation acquired a mechanism, and lost a defence

Duffy's Sunday blitz produced one genuinely new technical claim and one rhetorical one.

**New:** the routers' **ability to broadcast data was turned off**, and that is under investigation; a second issue was found at a radar facility in **Amarillo, Texas**, where the same fibre work had been done weeks earlier. Combined with the Arkansas copper-to-fibre job, that is a mechanism rather than a metaphor: a maintenance procedure with a documented second site.

## 3. Israel: the unity talks lasted an hour

Yashar and Likud negotiators met in Herzliya and were done inside roughly an hour, with no statement from either side. That is the answer to the question that has been hanging over the Norden/Ra'am affair all day: whether the election-interference allegation would be absorbed into a coalition bargain before anyone investigated it. A one-hour meeting that produces no communiqué is not a bargain; it is a position.

Netanyahu's office calls the Channel 12 report "false" and says Norden "fulfilled his civic duty" by passing criminal information to police. Eisenkot has called for an immediate investigation and pledged one. The card on formal process stays live and moves slightly up: with the escape route through a unity government narrowing, the remaining paths to resolving the affair run through the Attorney General or the police.

Worth noting precisely because it is a rebuttal rather than an insult.
