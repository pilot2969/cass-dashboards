## Lead: the appellate courts closed the door twice, and the letters went out anyway

Three days before the polls open, the Ninth Circuit refused — 2-1 — the administration's emergency request to make California segregate roughly 20,000 ballots tied to people the Justice Department calls "likely" noncitizens. It was the third court loss on the same request in a week (Carter's district ruling Wednesday, then the panel Friday), and the circuit adopted Carter's reasoning almost verbatim: there is "no feasible way to identify and segregate" those ballots without "potentially catastrophic effects on the entire system." The government is now expected to take it to the Supreme Court, where the calendar, not the merits, does most of the work — a grant that lands after Tuesday is a ruling that never has to be enforced.

In the same 24 hours, a second federal judge (Thompson) barred DOJ and DHS from continuing what the order described as a "campaign to intimidate, threaten, and coerce" election officials, and the Ninth Circuit let stand the block on DHS's voter-fraud field operation through Election Day. Then, Thursday and Friday, acting Deputy AG Trent McCotter sent near-identical letters to election officials in all fifty states warning of "up to a year in prison and a $1,000 fine" for failing to preserve records — citing a 1960 statute, naming no violation and no investigation. That is the tell: the instrument survived the injunction in letter form. The order stopped threats attached to a specific ballot dispute; it did not stop a general warning sent to every state in the country.

Read the sequence, not the individual filings. One state, one month: grand jury subpoenas to California registrars, a segregation motion, a special master, a criminal-referral drumbeat in Alaska, a fifty-state letter, and now three appellate losses. Volume is the message; the predicate has not moved. The reported conduct underneath all of it is still recruitment of a namesake candidate — recruiting, without coercion or inducement, on the most careful reading available.

## Secondary stack
