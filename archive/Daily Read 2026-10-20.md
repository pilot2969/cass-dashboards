**What happened.** French activists plan a second wave of protests on October 24 — 212 separate mobilizations. An investigation branded "Chaos Inc" counts 101 activist groups in nine countries and publishes a list of 54 organizations it blames for the French riots. The State Department warns the second weekend could bring worse destruction.

**Why it matters.** The riots started over cost of living and education, not religion — that's what independent fact-checking found. But the story being sold to English-speaking audiences is "Muslim extremists burning France," complete with a numbered enemy roster. The list is the product.

**The pattern.** Once unrest gets a brand name and an enemy count, facts stop mattering: the story now has villains, and villains can be exported. Expect French imagery in US campaign ads within days.

**What history says.** Red-scare and moral-panic campaigns have always run on published lists of subversives. Publishing the list is the point where a frame becomes a target list.

**Watching next.** Acte II turnout on October 24; whether any provocation is staged at it; and whether the "Chaos Inc" roster gets applied to American protest movements before November 3.

<summary>[09:35] DOJ charges 28 in California — the vote-fraud story gets its cast</summary>

**What happened.** The Justice Department charged 28 green card holders in California with illegally registering to vote or voting, and is separately suing the state to block roughly 20,000 ballots. The AP has fact-checked the Census Bureau report the White House keeps citing: it does not show the mass noncitizen voting the President claims happened in 2020.

**Why it matters.** Prosecutions filed two weeks before an election are how a narrative acquires names, faces and headlines. Whatever the merits of any single case, the aggregate effect is to pre-stock an election story.

**The pattern.** The election-fraud argument has been built in stages: "the polls are fake" (late September), "early voting is skewed" (mid-October), and now exhibits. An accusation that must be ready *before* the votes are counted.

**What history says.** Emergency politics rarely invents grievances in the moment; it assembles them from artifacts manufactured quietly in advance. The California suit is under court watch — the first ruling is the legal tell.

**Watching next.** A ruling on the ballot-blocking suit; whether DOJ expands to more states; whether the language shifts from "fake polls" to "fake count" once votes are being tallied.

### [09:52] Britain puts its jets over Saudi Arabia — the Houthi war goes multinational
**What happened.** The UK announced Royal Air Force Typhoon jets will fly "defensive" air patrols over Saudi Arabia to shield cities and infrastructure from Houthi attack. The planes won't strike Houthi targets, Britain says. Refueling support from last month is extended 90 more days, with transport aircraft and advisers going too.

**Why it matters.** A third foreign air force is now in the Saudi skies problem. Britain's entry came six weeks after repeated strikes on Abha airport and Riyadh's main airport — Saudi air defenses were visibly failing, and London is now underwriting the gap.

**The pattern.** "Defensive-only" deployments are how countries enter wars by installment: each step looks like the last one. The moment a patrol jet shoots down a Houthi missile, Britain is at war without a vote or an announcement.

**What history says.** Escalation in proxy wars has historically arrived through exactly this doorway — advisers, refueling, defensive screens — rather than declarations.

**Watching next.** The first RAF engagement of a Houthi projectile; whether Saudi Arabia's regional pact partners step up in parallel; and whether the US points to allied participation when strikes resume after the midterms.
