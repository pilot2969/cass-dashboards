## Lead: the mine replaced the drone, and the price refused the claim

Iran changed instrument today. The IRGC Navy said two more oil tankers struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz overnight while using what it called an "unauthorized southern route," putting the two-day total at **six tankers caught fire and sunk**. It named no vessel, no flag, no cargo and no crew. It added that the ships were operating "under US military guidance," which is a claim about American direction of commercial shipping and is the first time that charge has been attached to the mine campaign rather than to the drones.

**One of the six is independently corroborated; five are not.** UKMTO confirmed a vessel was struck and British maritime authorities reported a new tanker attack. Reuters and AP carried the IRGC statement and attributed it. That is the whole evidentiary base: one confirmed hull, six claimed sinkings, zero named ships.

**Then the price answered, and it answered no.** Brent settled below $95 — down on the day, its move attributed by traders to recovering Middle East exports and a supply surplus. A mining campaign that had actually closed the strait could not coexist with a falling crude price on the same afternoon. The market is not a truth-teller, but it is the only participant in this story with money at risk, and it treated six sinkings as an unverified claim. Hold that against the claim, not the other way round.

**The off-ramp is being built by the wrong two countries.** An Iranian lawmaker said Tehran and Muscat are near a final joint plan governing navigation through the strait — the same instrument that has been "in final stages" since August, and the one Washington wanted without tolls. Foreign Minister Araghchi goes to Muscat Tuesday, after a regional ministers' meeting in Islamabad. Iran is negotiating the rules of the waterway with the coast's other littoral state while mining it. That is not a contradiction: whoever writes the transit rules controls the strait without closing it.

**Threshold.** Node 2C requires a *sustained strait closure* with effect. A declared mined lane, one confirmed hull, five unnamed claims and a falling crude price do not meet it, and I am not scoring a number up on a two-day claim count. **Node 2C holds at 40** and the tension recorded on 15 November — that the proxy branch is arguably underpriced — stands unchanged.

**Bahrain, third claim.** The IRGC said six drones targeted the radar at al-Riffa; Bahrain's military says it intercepted all before impact. Three Iranian claims of striking American installations in five days, still zero confirmed damage and zero American deaths, and still no named operational phase.

Bedford's schedule-integrity claim is met on the public numbers. That closes the unresolved item from last night, and it closes in the administration's favour.

**The political story escalated instead.** Governor Hochul called on Duffy to resign or for the President to remove him "immediately," and asked for a congressional investigation. That is the first elected official to demand a head rather than a hearing, and it converts an agency failure into a personnel question. Duffy meanwhile pressed the $50 billion ask — up from $30 billion — on the argument that "this is an emergency," with no lifecycle cost estimate behind it, six weeks after GAO said the programme had none.

**Read the two facts together.** The outage is over and the secretary is under attack. That is not incoherence: an operational failure that resolves cleanly removes the evidence while leaving the appropriation and the audit finding intact. The fight from here is about $50 billion and a GAO baseline, not about Friday. Every hour the system runs normally makes the money easier to spend and the oversight harder to sustain.

## 3. Israel: the bloc moved from formation to legislation

The Arrangements Committee arithmetic — 63 to 50 with both Palestinian-led factions — has converted into a governing programme without a government. The Eisenkot bloc is moving to replace Speaker Amir Ohana, and if it does, intends to begin advancing legislation **as early as Wednesday to repeal the previous coalition's judicial overhaul.**

That is a different order of event from coalition talks. Repealing the overhaul is the one thing the 2023 street and the 2026 election were both about, and it can be done from the Knesset floor by a bloc that cannot yet form a cabinet. It also means the legislative clock and the formation clock are now separate.

**And the unity door appears to be closing from the other side.** A senior Eisenkot-bloc official was quoted saying there is no chance of a unity government after the PMO's "attempted coup" — the Norden affair. Haaretz reads the talks as a signalling exercise aimed at Eisenkot's own right-wing partners, not a real bargain. Eisenkot himself says Netanyahu cannot remain prime minister amid the alleged vote meddling.

**Effect on the card.** The escape route — the affair absorbed into a coalition bargain before anyone investigates it — narrows again. **norden-raam-formal-process 34 → 37**, on the same threshold as before: only an AG or State Attorney announcement, a police internal-affairs probe, or a resolution establishing a commission of inquiry satisfies it.

## 4. The AI trade: a board said no, and a company is going public into the wreckage

Untracked this morning, and the largest business event of the week.

**Nvidia's board is not considering Jensen Huang's return**, per CNBC and Reuters — the board is focused on the national security investigation into him. An online petition for his reinstatement has passed 180,000 signatures; Trump has said he "wouldn't mind"; Musk has weighed in. None of it moves the board. Nvidia fell to a 52-week low, led a second straight session of AI selling, and the S&P lost 1.92% for a third straight decline.

**And Anthropic prices Wednesday, trades Thursday as ANT** — an 825-million-share offering reportedly at $70–78, roughly $64.4 billion raised at up to a $1.4 trillion valuation, on a **net loss exceeding $104 billion in the nine months to September**. A $26 billion private placement has Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC buying non-voting shares at the IPO price.

**Why this belongs on a political wire.** The private placement is the load-bearing detail: the companies that sell Anthropic its compute are underwriting its float at the offer price. That is not a market discovering a price, it is a supply chain capitalising itself. It also arrives in the same week the parties that lost the midterms will oversee an AI build-out that voters rejected in 40 of 41 data-centre ballot measures, and the same week Nvidia reports earnings for the first time without the executive who built it.
