About this board

CASS is a public scoreboard for the health of American institutions — the courts, Congress, election offices, health agencies, the civil service, the military. It does three things, and holds itself to the same standard it applies:

  • It commits in advance. Every claim on this board is written down before it happens, dated, and paired with the specific thing that would prove it wrong.
  • It scores itself in public. When a call is right, that is logged. When we miss, the miss stays on the board. A record that only keeps its wins is a diary, not a test.
  • It measures, not vibes. Confidence numbers are explicit probabilities; lead time from forecast to outcome is tracked; the whole method is written out below.

Why: history may not repeat, but it rhymes. This board tests whether the rhyme is real — by naming, in plain language and before the fact, the conditions historians have seen before systemic breaks, and watching whether they form again.

📖 Data Dictionary — every field on this board, defined▾

Definitions written so a first-time reader can interpret any card without prior context. Inherited from the legacy Command Dashboard layout.

Confidence %
The board's probability estimate that the statement resolves as written. Rendered on the card's badge; the sparkline beside it shows how that number has moved over time. A flat line at a fixed value means no new evidence moved the estimate — itself information.
Card class
What kind of claim this is, which decides how it can be scored: EVENT (a dated occurrence happens or does not), THESIS (a structural claim tested against multiple consequences), MECHANISM (a claim about how something works), PERSISTENCE (a condition holds or breaks), ROLLING (thematic, no single observable outcome — not scored until a threshold action is registered).
Window opens
The date the scored window begins. Usually the registration date, but not always: a card can be registered early while its scoring window opens later. Expiry counts from here.
Window closes
The date the scored window ends. A card unresolved on this date is a miss by rule — it does not roll forward, and the window is never amended once the outcome is visible.
Resolution
How the card ended: hit, miss, or unresolved. Nothing else. A card still inside its window has no resolution — "still tracking" is not a value, and neither is "holding": a read that a condition is persisting belongs in tracking state, not here.
Resolved on
The date the confirmer or falsifier fired — which is not the same as the date the window closed, and is shown separately so a hit can be seen to land inside its own window.
Confirmation badge
A resolved card carries a badge in its own headline row: a check and CONFIRMED for a hit, a cross and MISSED for a miss, HELD OPEN while a card is past its window but still live. Amber and dashed means the card was registered the day it resolved, so it reports rather than forecasts. Unresolved cards carry no badge at all, because a live claim has nothing to confirm yet.
Lead delta
Days from the date the card was written to the date it resolved: the foreknowledge the call actually bought. A 56-day lead means the claim stood for 56 days before reality tested it. Printed on every resolved card with both dates, and summarised as a median in the confirmation ledger, which excludes same-day calls so four registrations on election day cannot inflate it.
Window margin
Days between resolution and the card’s own window close. Positive means it confirmed with time to spare; negative, printed as past window, means the card outlived its window before it could be scored. A late resolution is never folded back into the lead average.
Confirmation ledger
The instrument’s own report card, printed above the register: scored hit rate, median lead, how many cards confirmed inside the window they were given, and how many were same-day calls. It sits there so no hit rate on this board can be read without its lead times next to it.
Anchor grade (A–E)
How hard the claim is to score, and on what source. A: one public document settles it. B: two or more agreeing public sources. C: a measurable series with a stated threshold. D: inference from partial data. E: judgement the board does not pretend to automate. Recorded at registration, not revised after the outcome.
Anchor type
What the window is anchored to — a scheduled event, a statistical release, a legal deadline, or a class-default horizon. A window anchored to nothing is a wish.
Anchor source
The specific source the window and its threshold rest on, named at registration so a reader can check it.
Window basis
Why the window has these edges: the external schedule or threshold that sets them, rather than a round number chosen for convenience.
Expiry date
The date the card stops being scoreable as written. Normally the window close; for a ROLLING card it is a review date rather than a close, which is why the two are listed separately. Unresolved at expiry = miss.
Expiry basis
Why that expiry: what makes the claim unscoreable after it.
Grace horizon
How long after the window a late hit may still earn credit. Recorded at registration and only then.
Grace locked
Whether the grace horizon was fixed before the outcome was visible. True: a late hit may score as LATE. False: no grace — a card registered without it does not acquire a second chance later, and cards predating this rule are shown unlocked rather than quietly granted one.
Skill-eligible
Whether the card tests forecasting skill. False for ROLLING and thematic cards: they are published and dated, but scoring them against a closing date would measure the calendar rather than the claim, so they score nothing.
Tracking state
A read on how a live card is behaving before it resolves — the condition persisting, or drifting away from the stated mechanism. Informational. It never substitutes for a resolution and cannot be cited as one.
Confirmer
The pre-registered condition that resolves the card as a hit. Named before the outcome; if it cannot be named at registration, the card is not scoreable yet.
Confirmer base rate
How often the confirmer would fire on its own, by chance or on its ordinary baseline. This is what stops a common event being scored as a successful forecast.
Confirmation window
The span in which the confirmer counts. A confirmer that fires outside it does not resolve the card as a hit.
Threshold definition
The numeric cut that converts a measurable series into a hit or a miss, stated before the series is read.
Falsifier
The specific, dated, observable condition that would kill or resolve the prediction. Written before the outcome is known. If the falsifier is ambiguous, the prediction is not instrument-grade. Conjunctive falsifiers — every prong must fire before the card dies — are flagged, because one dead prong can leave a claim standing forever.
Outcome direction
Which way the card resolves: whether the predicted thing happening counts as a hit, or whether the card is a call that something will not happen. Set at registration so direction cannot be reinterpreted afterwards.
Quarantine reason
Why a card is withheld from the public register — withdrawn, superseded, or held back for cause. A quarantined card is still scored and the reason stays on the record.
Public status
Whether the card is public, internal, or published ahead of the adjudication gate. Marks the cards whose statement should be read with that caveat.
Instrument
The data series or measure the card is scored against, and where it comes from.
Revision log
Each material change to the card, with date and reason. A card revised after its outcome was visible would be visible here — which is the point of keeping it.
Baseline retrofit
Marks a card whose base rate was reconstructed after registration from historical data, and records which part was fitted late.
Confidence rationale
The reasoning behind the number at registration: what raised it, what held it back, and how the scale was read. Recorded so a confidence can be audited later against the evidence that existed when it was set, rather than reconstructed from the outcome.
Correlation flag
Marks a card that is not an independent draw from its siblings — it resolves on the same underlying variable as another card, or shares an event stream, a sensor or a source with one. Correlated cards are scored once, on the owning card; the product of their individual probabilities is not the joint probability of all of them, and agreement between two of them is never counted as two confirmations.
Resolution note
How the outcome was reached: which confirmer or falsifier fired, on what source, and any judgement call made in reading it. Set at resolution and left in place, so the arithmetic behind a hit or a miss stays visible after the fact.
Max lifespan
The longest this card may stay open before it expires regardless of its window, so a claim cannot stay live indefinitely by being unfalsifiable today.
Review cadence
How often a live card is re-read while it is open.
Status: pending
Statement is live; no falsifier has triggered and no resolution date has arrived. The status field describes position in the lifecycle; the resolution field records the outcome. They are reported separately and never merged.
Evidence confirmed
Facts on the record that support the statement. Listed itemized so each can be independently checked.
Evidence contradicted
Facts on the record that cut against the statement. Logged with equal prominence — the board's honesty test.
Watch (forward)
Named signals to monitor next — the specific events that would move confidence up or down.
Statement
The claim itself, exactly as registered and scored. Not edited to fit the outcome: a claim that needed changing is revised openly and the change appears in the revision log.
Title
The card's short name for display. Titles repeat across sibling cards on the same subject, which is why every card is keyed internally by its own ID rather than its title.
Card ID
The card's permanent identifier. Counts, deduplication and drift checks all key on this, so no card can be double-counted and none can silently vanish.
Registered
The date the card was registered. Commitments made at this date are what the board is held to; the window is a separate matter.
Time (ET)
Time of registration or resolution, in Eastern Time, where a same-day sequence matters.
Status: emergent
Partial confirmations are accumulating but the statement's full resolution criteria are not yet met — the mechanism is visibly underway.
Status: confirmed
The falsifier's symmetric criteria have been met — the event happened as specified, on record.
Status: failed
The falsifier triggered against the prediction. Logged publicly — misses stay on the board.
⏱ lead time
Interval from the log date to the resolution date, shown on resolved entries. The board's calibration record.
Macro Forecast
The board's single highest-conviction systemic call. One at a time; falsifiers armed; retired publicly if triggered.
MGET Ledger
Running log of mechanism tests: each entry records a real-world event scored against the model's legs (complexity cap, elite overproduction, enclosure mutation, vanguard alignment). Verdicts: confirms / strains / undermines / neutral.
MGET legs
The four structural legs of the model being tested by each ledger entry. An entry may test one or several.
Forward signal
What the ledger entry implies to watch next — feeding the next day's scan.
Sparkline
Per-card confidence trend line from history.json — daily snapshots, oldest to newest, red if rising, green if falling. Hover for dated values.
Watch (term)
A Watch is NOT a prediction. It means conditions are primed — if a spark lands, it will travel fast.
Last moved
The date the confidence score was last changed, separate from the registration date. A card that has not moved since registration is not being ignored — it means no new evidence met the standard for a revision, and that stasis is information. Used by the movers check to separate live cards from cards that have gone quiet.
Base rate (numeric)
The reference-class frequency for the predicted event, expressed as a number where the prose confirmer base rate would otherwise be a judgement. Stored numerically so the register can compare a stated confidence against the rate the same event happens on its own. Where the two are close, the card is measuring the baseline rather than skill.
Tribunal case
The Gauntlet docket ID under which the claim was attacked, if it has been. Links a forecast to its adversarial hearing, where the claim is tested for testability rather than for being right. A blank field means the claim has not yet been docketed, which is not the same as having survived.
Tribunal verdict
Ruling of the adversarial panel on the claim, not on the forecast: SUSTAINED (no material concession), REVISED (materially changed), OVERTURNED, HELD FOR REVISION (no falsification test stated, so not yet scoreable), PENDING (not yet heard), VOID (improperly constituted). A verdict says whether a claim can be tested; it is never added to a hit rate.
MGET leg
Which of the four model gauges this card is filed under — Material, Governance, Enclosure or Turning, or N when the claim is not a leg claim at all. A classifier, not a score: it says which engine the card is watching, and the Model State panel counts the register by it. Where cards pile up is not where the evidence is, which is why the panel prints the distribution.
Secondary leg
A second gauge the card also bears on, where one exists. Recorded rather than inferred, so a card is never counted twice in the distribution. The primary leg is the one the card can actually resolve; the secondary is context.
Leg classification basis
Why the card was filed under that gauge — the specific mechanism in the claim that belongs to Material, Governance, Enclosure or Turning. Written at classification so the assignment can be argued with rather than just discovered.
Base rate source
Where the reference-class frequency came from: the named historical series, the number of cases in it, and how they were counted. A base rate with no stated source is an opinion in numeric form.
Base rate tier
How strong the reference class is. A: a coded series with a stated n and uniform inclusion rule. B: a series assembled by hand with a stated rule. C: an analogy set — comparable in shape, not in kind. D: no base rate established, and the card says so rather than implying one.
Base rate frozen
The date the reference class was closed. Anyone reading the number later can see which cases it could and could not have included — so a base rate cannot quietly absorb the outcome it is supposed to be tested against.
Derivation gap
What stands between the base rate and the stated confidence, said out loud rather than buried in the number. If the card is 20 points above its reference class, this field names the specific evidence doing that work. An empty gap means the confidence is the base rate plus nothing.
Post-expiry confirmation
Whether the predicted event happened after the card had already expired as a miss. Recorded because the register scores the claim as written, not the analyst's intent — a late confirmation is real information about the world and zero credit on the card. Both facts stay on the record.
Grace lock note
The reasoning behind the grace decision, recorded at registration: whether a late hit may score, for how long, and on what basis. Grace granted after an outcome is visible is not grace; it is a rewrite.
Grace lock date
The date the grace decision was recorded. Grace counts only if it was locked before the window closed and before any outcome was visible; a grace horizon written down later is a rewrite and is not granted, however convenient it would be. Where this date is absent the horizon was never locked.
Substance probability
The share of a card's stated confidence that rests on the demonstrated mechanism rather than on the timing. Shown separately on disjunctive and fork cards so a reader can see which half of the number is doing the work.
Timing probability
The share of a card's stated confidence that rests on the event landing inside its window. Substance without timing is a true claim that misses; timing without substance is a coin landing on its edge.
Deadline type
What makes the card's window hard: constitutional (the date is fixed by law and cannot move), scheduled (a calendar release or session date), statistical (a series publication), or class-default (the horizon the class itself implies). A constitutional deadline cannot be extended by anyone, which is why cards built on one are graded more confidently than cards built on a horizon.
Successor card
The card that supersedes this one when it is retired or revised into a narrower claim. A retiring card is not deleted: it is closed and pointed at its successor, so a failed claim can never be quietly replaced by a new one without the record showing the handover.
Falsifier shape audit
The check that a card's falsifier is single-pronged and observable before the outcome is visible. Conjunctive falsifiers are flagged, because every prong must fire before the card can die and one dead prong leaves the claim standing forever. Disjunctive confirmers get the same treatment. A card that fails this audit is not scoreable as written.
Model State
The panel above Institutional Stress. It reads the four MGET legs — Material, Governance, Enclosure, Turning — and reports two things per leg: state (what the leg’s own operational test says) and load (how heavy the conditions enabling it are). The panel is not a forecast: it says which part of the system is generating present-day pressure, not what happens next. Cards are filed under a leg as a classifier, never as a contribution.
Leg M — Material (leg 1, complexity cap)
Tainter's complexity cap, read through the material substrate: fiscal stress, energy and supply fragility, administrative slack. Its operational test: a critical flow is physically constrained and the constraint has already transmitted into price or quantity. M can be fired while markets look calm and loaded while it is not fired; the two readings are deliberately kept apart. Sub-mechanism on the record: two-way chokepoint saturation — closure blocks exports and the spare parts needed to end the closure.
Leg G — Governance (leg 2, elite overproduction)
Turchin's elite overproduction, read as the state's capacity and legitimacy to act. It is not hand-set: the reading is the mean of the eight Institutional Stress dials, so the two panels cannot disagree. High G means the state is spending legitimacy faster than it banks it — the condition that turns a policy failure into a regime question. The live diagnostic is the bankruptcy-wave question: who absorbs the wreckage.
Leg E — Enclosure (leg 3, enclosure mutation)
Polanyi's enclosure mutation: the conversion of shared capacity into private or closed holdings, and the mutation of that enclosure into new forms. The signal is not that enclosure exists — it always does — but that its form changes: a chokepoint becomes a financial instrument, a public function becomes a rent stream, a security guarantee migrates away from the umbrella that issued it. Enclosure mutation is what makes the other legs unaffordable for everyone outside the enclosure.
Leg T — Turning (leg 4, vanguard alignment)
The discriminant. Its operational test is strict on purpose: a civilian corps or parallel apparatus holding executive loyalty without independent command, capable of acting on its own initiative. Absent that, no amount of generalised alienation counts as a vanguard — which is why T can read heavy load with leg 4 absent. In the corpus, the two-stage theorem runs legs 1–3 as the warning tremor and leg 4 as the detonation: three legs fired without leg 4 is historically a rupture channelled rather than a regime break.
Load
100 × the mean of four named anchors per leg, each scored 0–5 on one fixed scale: 0 no strain; 1 ordinary friction within normal political economy; 2 strain visible in a named series or record, not yet transmitted into behaviour; 3 strain arrived in household budgets, prices or institutional behaviour; 4 strain is the binding constraint, actors are choosing around it; 5 capacity exhausted — rationing, default, closure or open refusal. An anchor must cite the observable that produced it; an anchor with no citation cannot score above 2. Weights are equal, which is a design choice and not a finding. Bands (0–39 slack, 40–59 straining, 60–74 fired, 75–100 at capacity) are display only and can never produce a state.
State (FIRED / ABSENT / CONTESTED)
The leg’s own test, read as a yes-or-no. FIRED: the operational threshold has been met and is on record. ABSENT: the thing the test names has not been observed — not "not yet", just not observed. CONTESTED: evidence cuts both ways. This is a different object from load: load measures the pressure feeding a leg, state measures whether the leg has crossed its threshold. A gauge cannot show both in one number, so the panel shows both.
Gate (3 of 4)
The model’s rupture condition: three of four legs fired. Two legs fired is a serious period that resolves within the order; three is the historical signature of a rupture. Leg 4 is the discriminant — when three fire and leg 4 holds, the historical record shows rupture channelled into renewal rather than collapse.
Lane prices
How the forecast book is currently distributed across the four possible outcomes of the period, read live from the four branch cards that partition it and summing to 100. Not a single card’s confidence: it is the book’s weight across mutually exclusive lanes, and the sync warns if the lanes stop summing to 100.
Register load (count)
How many cards are filed under each leg. This measures where the book is looking, not where pressure is. A leg holding 39 cards is not thereby more loaded; a leg holding 9 is not thereby quiet. Printed on the panel precisely so the distinction stays visible.
📖 Methodology — how this board is built and how it is tested▾
How we test claims. Every forecast is written so it can be proven wrong. A panel kept separate from the author attacks each claim, and the claim only survives if the evidence holds. Rulings are public and there are exactly four: SUSTAINED (the claim survived with no material concession), REVISED (the claim was materially changed — the expected outcome for a good thesis, and never a pass by default), OVERTURNED, and HELD FOR REVISION (no falsification test was stated, so the claim cannot be scored yet). Two further outcomes apply to the case itself rather than the claim: PENDING (not yet heard by an independent panel) and VOID (improperly constituted). A verdict is a statement about a claim's testability; it is not a forecast outcome, and the two are never added together.
Rule 1 — every forecast expires. Each card carries a window with two edges (window_opens, window_closes) and an expiry_date. A card that is still unresolved when its window closes is a miss. Not a hold, not a "still tracking" — a miss, scored and left on the board. Grace exists only where it was locked at registration (grace_locked); it has never been granted retroactively to a card whose window already closed, and no window may be amended once its outcome is visible. Pre-expiry persistence reads ("the blockade is holding") are recorded in tracking_state and are not resolutions.
What counts as a hit — under review. The board currently scores one number: did the event happen inside the window. A forecast that was right but late therefore scores identically to one that never fired, which we do not believe is the final answer. The proposed replacement separates substance (was the mechanism real, as described) from timing (did it arrive inside the window): substance earns the credit, lateness discounts it, and a pre-registered horizon still kills the card. That proposal was filed with the tribunal and returned HELD FOR REVISION — it has not been adopted, and this page will say so plainly when and if it is. The objection it must answer: if timing stops mattering, no claim can ever miss, and a claim that cannot miss is not a forecast.
Card classes and anchor grades. Every card states its class — EVENT (a dated occurrence either happens or does not), THESIS (a structural claim tested against multiple observable consequences), MECHANISM (a claim about how something works), PERSISTENCE (a condition holds or breaks), or ROLLING (thematic; no single observable outcome, so not scored until a threshold action is registered against it). Every card also carries an anchor grade A–E, recording how hard the claim is to score and against what source — an A can be settled by one public document, an E requires judgement that the board is not pretending to automate. An unanchored card is not a forecast; it is a note.
Nothing ships unreviewed. A single command renders every page on this site headless, executes its own scripts against the live data layer, and asserts what a reader would see before and after each push: ledger freshness and count, register count and class labels, edition date, panel failures, leaked tokens, and whether the deployed bytes match the build. A failing check blocks the push. Pushed, deployed and verified are three different facts and are never reported as one; the board is called updated only when the deployed site itself passes.
The MGET framework. The board runs on a four-leg model of systemic failure: Leg 1 — complexity cap / material substrate (energy, logistics, money); Leg 2 — elite overproduction / class friction; Leg 3 — enclosure mutation (institutions becoming opaque, weaponized, or unaccountable); Leg 4 — vanguard alignment (coordinated counter-elites forming). Each leg is scored 0–15 from visible, public evidence only.
Forecasts are bets on mechanisms, not dates. Each entry names its mechanism, its evidence base, and its falsifier — the specific condition that would retire it. When a falsifier triggers, the forecast is retired publicly, on this page. A forecast that cannot be falsified is not a forecast; it is a mood.
Lead-time measurement. Every forecast shows the date it was logged and the date it resolved. The gap between the two is our lead time — measured, not claimed.
The Threshold Watch. The Watch is the board's public storm signal. A Watch does not say an event is happening; it says conditions are primed. It carries an expiry date, is re-checked weekly, and can only do three things: upgrade, hold, or expire. It never quietly lapses.
Blind backtesting. The model is validated by reconstructing past eras using only period-available information, then scoring the call against what actually happened. The protocol requires three passes: correct mechanism class, directional macro call, and nulls produced where nulls were warranted. To date, seven macro-instrument eras and one counter-case battery have been tested (the core engine's headline suite), plus a separate six-run policy/regulatory track reported apart from the core number:
• Nov 2019 (pre-corridor): board printed 22%, no Watch — correct null.
• Sept 2001 (exogenous shock): macro silent at 12%; the vanguard leg caught the shock class at 35–40%.
• Sept 2008 (corridor detonation): board printed ~70%, Watch issued; detonation landed inside the stated 30-day window.
• Full historical sweep (n=8): Reformation 1517, Glorious Revolution 1688, French Revolution 1787, 1848 revolutions, 1968 counterculture, plus three control eras — legs scored on period wire only. Score: 6 of 8 clean PASS, 1 PARTIAL, 1 MISSED.
• Counter-case battery: Soviet 1965, Singapore 1990, China 2000 — raw opacity shows ~zero conversion to decay timing; Leg 3's mutation signal, not opacity itself, carries the timing power.
Full protocol: CASS Blind Backtest Protocol (Standing Methodology), Samuel Adams, 2026, cited 2026-09-22. Headline sample: n=3 original + n=8 historical sweep + 3 counter-cases (14 eras; 4 PASS, 1 STRAIN, 1 MISS, 1 counter-case rejection class). A secondary track of 6 policy/regulatory blind runs (Basel III, GDPR, Medicaid expansion, No Surprises Act, Oregon Measure 110, PPACA subsidies) is reported separately and never mixed into the headline number. Known limits are stated in the protocol, including the risk of outcome contamination in self-graded runs and the provenance caveat that sweep eras are reconstructions rather than true blind runs. One falsifier-triggered failure (PPACA enrollment) is honestly logged on the secondary track. The Falsifiability Collapse Protocol — governing claims structured so that falsifying evidence is systematically unobservable — is a standing clause. Per-era leg scores and grades are recorded in the MGET control ledger.
