## 7. The World Court found thousands killed in Myanmar, and that is an artifact

**The facts.** The International Court of Justice found that Myanmar forces killed thousands of Rohingya, with the ruling ongoing (6 outlets).

**Why it matters.** It is the only document produced this week that cannot be argued with by press release. A court finding is a dated, citable artifact with a named respondent — the same class of thing as a cleared docket or a withdrawn certification, and the corpus runs short of them. Everything else in this sweep is a claim by an interested party; this is a finding by a body with jurisdiction.

**What history says.** The Gambia filed in 2019 and the provisional measures were unanimous and ignored. A merits finding does not enforce itself; the enforcement instrument is the Security Council, where the respondent's patrons sit. So the base rate for a merits ruling changing behaviour is low and the base rate for it changing the record is one hundred per cent.

**The pattern worth naming.** The international legal layer is still emitting documents while domestic institutions emit injunctions. That is not a compliment to either; it is an observation about which layer still has a procedure that terminates.
